## 1. Why PLCs fail, become "PLC lite", compliance paperwork or "meetings about meetings"

### Takeaway
The failure practitioners name most often is a team that has the form of a PLC (scheduled meetings, agendas, forms) without the substance: the team's discussions never lead to changes in teaching based on student evidence. Teachers' biggest complaints are meetings that turn into admin or staff meetings, data and forms that are collected for compliance, and lack of time.

### Cited Findings
- DuFour & Reeves, "The Futility of PLC Lite" (*Phi Delta Kappan* 97(6), March 2016). Their definition of the genuine process: collaborative teams, a guaranteed and viable curriculum, formative assessments drawn from that curriculum, and using the results to inform interventions and teaching — [ERIC EJ1091902](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1091902)
- They define PLC Lite as "when educators rename their traditional faculty or department meetings as PLC meetings, engage in book studies that result in no action, or devote collaborative time to topics that have no effect on student achievement—all in the name of the PLC process." The quote was reached through secondary sources — [Solution Tree handout (Muhammad, "Leadership Matters: Moving Beyond PLC Lite")](https://solutiontree.s3.amazonaws.com/solutiontree.com/media/pdf/handouts/leadership/Muhammad-LeadershipMatters.pdf); [Solution Tree Blog, "Don't Stop at PLC Lite"](https://www.solutiontree.com/blog/dont-stop-at-plc-lite/)
- "What separates accountable teams from PLC Lite teams" is that they "change their practice based on what the student data is showing them" — [Solution Tree Blog, "Don't Stop at PLC Lite"](https://www.solutiontree.com/blog/dont-stop-at-plc-lite/)
- An AllThingsPLC post, "Avoiding the PLC Lite Scenario", describes teams with the structure of a PLC (regular meetings, an agenda) but none of the substance. The test it gives is whether discussions lead to instructional changes — [AllThingsPLC blog](https://allthingsplc.secure.agroup.com/blog/view/301/what-is-a-model-plc-school)
- Later researchers have applied the concept to PLCs that stay "at the discourse level, or in superficial changes in the working practices" — [Uruguay ITE PLC study (SciELO 2024)](http://www.scielo.edu.uy/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S1688-93042024000101201)
- In BCG's 2015 teacher survey, more than half of teachers were dissatisfied with the collaboration they get through PLCs. Even when time is allocated, it "often devolves into an administrative staff meeting." Teachers still want to collaborate and name protected time as the fix — [BCG, "How to Help Teachers Get Better Together" (2015)](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2015/education-public-sector-how-to-help-teachers-get-better-together)
- Reforms "invariably entail added meetings, extensive planning documents, new reporting." Nevada principals estimated about 19 work days a year on paperwork for a new evaluation system — [Education Next, "Educators' Time Loss and the Invisible Cost of Reform"](https://www.educationnext.org/educators-time-loss-invisible-cost-reform/). This concerns evaluation paperwork, not PLCs specifically, but it is the same compliance dynamic.
- Data overload: PLCs should work with district leaders to align data-collection requirements with realistic goals, "shifting the emphasis from compliance to meaningful action" — ["Tackling Data Overload in PLCs" (blog)](https://dog-smilodon-f7zs.squarespace.com/blog-2-1/tackling-data-overload-in-plcs)
- ERIC studies: insufficient time for quality discussion was a main challenge, and some teachers saw PLC time "as competing with their other teaching work" — [ERIC ED596744 (Singapore)](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED596744.pdf). Limited time was the biggest barrier and "lack of teacher buy-in was the second greatest" — [ERIC EJ1137497](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1137497.pdf). Another study named lack of time, lack of shared leadership and lack of communication — [ERIC search result (barriers study)](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED563128). Participants "spoke negatively of the Data Teams in terms of time," citing a narrow focus on one set of metrics — [ERIC search summary, EJ1054892](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1054892.pdf)
- Trust is critical to implementing the PLC model, and the principal is best placed to influence it. Successful and unsuccessful PLCs described trust differently — [Hallam et al., NASSP Bulletin 2015, ERIC EJ1074734](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1074734)
- Commercial templates sell sign-in sheets so staff are held accountable "for their attendance and participation in PLC meetings." This is an example of the surveillance-style documentation teachers associate with compliance — [TpT listings](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/browse?search=plc+documentation)
- An Instruction Partners author argues that "PLC" has become "any conversation among any group of educators about anything relevant to students" — [Instruction Partners, "Demystifying Professional Learning" (2022)](https://instructionpartners.org/2022/05/23/demystifying-professional-learning/)
- ASCD's "Making Protocols Work" quotes a teacher describing meetings that are engaging but "never seem to build toward anything" — [ASCD](https://www.ascd.org/el/articles/making-protocols-work)

### Inferences
- The documents teachers experience as busywork are the ones that face upward to administrators (sign-in sheets, compliance minutes, data reports nobody acts on). The documents they value face inward and feed the team's next decision. An app should make every field feed a later step, such as an essential standard becoming a CFA, a CFA result becoming an intervention group, and an intervention becoming a next step.
- A "did we change instruction?" check is the core anti-PLC-lite signal, and a light prompt at the end of each cycle could capture it.

### Gaps
- I could not access the full text of DuFour & Reeves or the AllThingsPLC blog post.
- I found no systematic survey that ranks which PLC documents teachers find most burdensome (norms forms, SMART goal forms, minutes and so on). The evidence is anecdotal or comes from consultancies.

## 2. What teams find most valuable in the process

### Takeaway
Across sources, the high-value activities are: agreeing on a small set of essential ("must-know") standards and unwrapping only those; writing and giving a common formative assessment (CFA); looking at actual student work together with a protocol; and turning results into specific reteaching and intervention moves.

### Cited Findings
- The AllThingsPLC glossary describes team-developed common formative assessments as "the lynchpin" of the PLC process — [AllThingsPLC Terms (PDF)](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf)
- Teams should unwrap only essential standards. Unwrapping gives teams a way to reach agreement on "what the standard is really trying to say," and Solution Tree calls it "a priceless professional development opportunity" — [Solution Tree reproducible](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_EYT/unwrappingthestandardsapricelessprofessionaldevelopmentopportunity.pdf)
- Start with students' "must knows" before unwrapping — [AllThingsPLC, "Understanding the 'What'"](https://www.allthingsplc.info/blog/view/397/understanding-the-what)
- Solution Tree's Looking at Student Work protocol has four steps. It starts by naming the question the team is trying to answer, requires agreement on scoring beforehand, and takes about 60 minutes — [Solution Tree reproducible fig. 4.7](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_MCCPLCW/figure4.7lookingatstudentworkprotocolinstructions.pdf)
- HMH describes a related sequence: teachers give the same assessment on the same day and bring a broad range of student work samples to the meeting — [HMH blog](https://www.hmhco.com/blog/getting-into-math-looking-at-student-work-to-improve-instruction)
- Edutopia's Analyzing Student Work protocol includes a stage where peers discuss their assessment of the task and the work, and solutions — [Edutopia](https://www.edutopia.org/practice/analyze-student-work-inform-instruction)
- Use CFA results to "get past the standard to the exact skill" students missed, and change the approach if the first reteach does not work — [Solution Tree Blog, "Formative Assessment Strategies to Build PLC Interventions"](https://www.solutiontree.com/blog/formative-assessment-strategies-to-build-plc-interventions/)
- Teachers in a 2025 study prized peer collaboration focused on curriculum and pedagogy and said it led to lasting changes in practice — [Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42330-025-00367-z)
- DuFour's test for "doing data right" is whether the use of data leads to improved student learning — [ASCD Educational Leadership, "How PLCs Do Data Right" (2015)](https://www.ascd.org/el/articles/how-plcs-do-data-right)
- An evidence review rates PLCs' impact on teachers as "mixed" and on pupils as "promising" — [NIoT Evidence Portal](https://evidenceportal.niot.org.uk/toolkit/teacher-collaboration/professional-learning-communities/)

### Inferences
- An app's spine should be: essential standard, then unwrapped learning targets, then CFA, then results and student work, then response (reteach, intervene, extend). Supporting artifacts such as norms and roles should be lightweight and stay in the background.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative ranking from teachers of which steps they find most valuable.

## 3. Time: fitting the work into limited collaboration time; agendas, cycles and calendars; continuity

### Takeaway
The typical pattern is a protected weekly block of about 45 minutes (sometimes biweekly or longer early-release blocks) with a fixed, timed agenda that ends with owners and dates. Several district tools tie each meeting to one of the four critical questions. Continuity comes from agendas sent ahead, rotating roles (facilitator, recorder, timekeeper), and a "next meeting agenda / next steps" item. I found no standard published 3- or 4-week cycle map.

### Cited Findings
- DoDEA guidance calls for a consistent "sacred" PLC time, with at least one meeting a week of at least 45 minutes — [DoDEA PLC Guidance (2017)](https://content.dodea.edu/teach_learn/ids/product_showcase/docs/guides/professional_learning_communities_guidance_final_02072017.pdf)
- Most effective PLCs meet 30–60 minutes at a time. A grade-level team meeting 45 minutes weekly is typical — [Otus guide](https://otus.com/resources/guides/plcs-in-education) (vendor)
- Schools build PLC time into the master schedule. Examples are rotating early dismissal, and shortened class blocks on PLC days to create a 38-minute weekly block — [Scheduling Practices for PLCs (older district document)](https://www.wfbschools.com/cms_files/resources/Scheduling%20Practices%20for%20Professional%20Learning%20Communities.pdf)
- PLCs "can't meaningfully happen in 10 minutes between classes" — [Collective Learning blog](https://www.collectivelearning.info/blog/professional-learning-communities-strategies)
- A timed 45-minute template runs warm-up, student work, plan, commit, aiming to leave with a next-day instructional plan. The same source suggests meeting weekly to build momentum, then biweekly once next-day moves are consistently implemented — [Solved Consulting, PLC Agenda Templates 30/45/60](https://www.solvedconsulting.com/blog/plc-agenda-templates-30-45-60-minutes-doc) (consultant)
- Wisconsin DPI's protocol is organized around the four PLC questions and assumes 60–90 minutes plus follow-up, which is longer than the 45-minute norm — [WI DPI PLC Protocol](https://dpi.wi.gov/sites/default/files/imce/wisexplore/PLC_Protocol.pdf)
- A district model: core teams meet every other week (at least twice a month) on the same day district-wide, documented on a shared calendar organized by week number. Teams fill in the agenda by the Friday before, including norms, the meeting goal and which of the four PLC questions the meeting addresses — [district slide deck (Google Slides)](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1tU2Fsp-_rL0Zy8pQADgBARCeCp05rtkT/htmlpresent)
- Solution Tree's multi-school PLC calendar example assigns a PLC lead, time and location to each team on a weekly grid — [Solution Tree reproducible, fig. 29](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_MSCT/figure29examplemsctplccalendar.pdf)
- New Jersey publishes a PLC year-long cycle document — [NJ AchieveNJ PLC Year-Long Cycle](https://www.state.nj.us/education/AchieveNJ/teams/strat14/PLCYearLongCycle.pdf) (contents not read)
- A teacher writing in Edutopia says pacing and school-calendar events (testing, field trips) should be filled in on a calendar before the meeting, which "saves valuable meeting time" — [Edutopia, "Overcoming Interpersonal Challenges in PLCs"](https://www.edutopia.org/article/overcoming-interpersonal-challenges-in-plcs)
- Edutopia's "5 Ways to Make PLCs Work Better": specific agendas with clear objectives; digital agendas where members attach student work, assessments or lesson plans; PLC time treated as "sacred" with disruptions only for true emergencies; stipends for teacher facilitators; principal "visiting hours" — [Edutopia](https://www.edutopia.org/article/5-ways-make-plcs-work-better/)
- WestEd's VITAL tool includes a school-year planner, a meeting planner with objectives and roles, and a next-steps item: "Outline an agenda for the team's next meeting" — [WestEd VITAL PLC Scheduling and Agenda Tool](https://vital.wested.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/VITAL_Tool_PLC-Scheduling-and-Agenda-Tool.docx); [WestEd blog, "Moving PLCs From Planning to Learning"](https://www.wested.org/blog/insights-impact/moving-plcs-from-planning-to-learning-the-vital-approach/)
- Rotating roles: facilitator (creates the agenda), recorder (keeps minutes and maintains a shared digital folder of PLC data and resources), timekeeper, reporter — [Study.com, PLC Group Roles & Norms](https://study.com/academy/lesson/how-plcs-create-group-norms.html). Rotating the facilitator builds leadership — [Edutopia search results](https://www.edutopia.org/article/setting-up-effective-plc/)
- Agendas sent a week ahead so members can review them and add items — [search summary of PLC agenda sources](https://csaa.wested.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/PLC_Meeting_Agenda_d.pdf)
- readbyexample Substack (partial excerpt only): the author's school meets weekly on Wednesday afternoons for close to two hours. The author asks how leaders can tell whether collaborative skills are developing and used PLC time to prepare for curriculum-development PD. Final recommendations were not visible — [Read by Example, "Leading PLCs: How should we structure our time?"](https://readbyexample.substack.com/p/leading-plcs-how-should-we-structure)

### Inferences
- Since no canonical 3- or 4-week cycle surfaced, the following design is my inference. Map a unit cycle to the four questions. Meeting 1: what must they learn (essential standard, targets, proficiency criteria) and how will we know (draft or agree on the CFA). Meeting 2: finalize the CFA and agree on scoring. Meeting 3: after the CFA, look at results and student work. Meeting 4: respond (interventions and extensions, share strategies, record what changed). This fits 45-minute meetings and could be shown as a calendar.
- For continuity, an app can automatically carry each meeting's "next steps (owner, date)" into the next agenda, which replicates WestEd's "outline next agenda" step without extra writing.
- Calendar events and pacing should be entered outside meeting time, and the app could pre-fill them.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous study comparing meeting lengths or cadences for effectiveness.
- I could not read the readbyexample Substack conclusions.

## 4. Documenting meetings, discussions and decisions without burden

### Takeaway
Useful documentation is brief and decision-focused: the agenda and the results in one place, with decisions and owners/dates captured before the meeting ends, and student work and data attached rather than retyped. Heavy minutes and attendance logs are associated with compliance.

### Cited Findings
- "The minutes role ensures owners/dates are captured before the meeting ends." A "simple minutes format" accompanies the 30/45/60 templates — [Solved Consulting](https://www.solvedconsulting.com/blog/plc-agenda-templates-30-45-60-minutes-doc)
- The recorder documents each topic, discussion points and decisions on a minutes template as the discussion happens — [Athlos, "Virtual PLCs"](https://amac.athlos.org/virtual-plcs-staying-connected-with-your-team/)
- A popular teacher-made template puts "the agenda and the documentation of results all in one place, while keeping the focus on the 4 critical questions" — [TpT listing](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/browse?search=plc+meeting+template)
- Record norms in a shared document and review them at the start of each meeting. Each member shares responsibility for holding others accountable — [Guide to Establishing PLC Norms](https://core-docs.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/4517/BAPS/4362641/Guide_to_Establishing_PLC_Norms.pdf)
- Solution Tree's *Big Book of Tools for Collaborative Teams* (Ferriter) has over 100 tools organized around the four critical questions, including team roles, meeting notes and task tracking — [Solution Tree](https://www.solutiontree.com/free-resources/plcbooks/bbtct)
- Solution Tree's sample collaborative team meeting agenda template covers date, team and attendees — [Solution Tree reproducible](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_CTTW/samplecollaborativeteammeetingagendatemplate.pdf)
- A Solution Tree facilitator's guide featuring Many includes a "Generate Products" step: teams create products related to adult and student learning — [Collaborative Teams in a PLC at Work Facilitator's Guide](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/collaborative-teams-in-plc-at-work-facilitators-guide-072122.pdf)

### Inferences
- In the app, a meeting record could be three short fields (decisions; next steps with owner and date; questions to carry forward) plus attachments. The point is to avoid narrative minutes.
- Many's "products" idea suggests that the artifacts (the standards list, the CFA, the intervention plan) are the real documentation. Minutes can stay minimal.

### Gaps
- I found no study measuring how much time teachers spend on PLC documentation.

## 5. Sharing instructional strategies based on results (teacher-by-teacher comparison)

### Takeaway
The DuFour/AllThingsPLC model explicitly expects teams to look at results teacher by teacher so they can learn from the colleague whose students did best, for example by having that teacher reteach a group or share lessons. Practitioners warn that this can cause anxiety or "comparison contests". The usual mitigations are explicit data norms, factual (not interpretive) data review, collective framing, and invitation-based observation. I found no source that explicitly endorses "our students" wording or a formal opt-in sharing protocol tied to CFA results.

### Cited Findings
- ASCD: a teacher who is succeeding with a skill can share lesson plans, have colleagues observe, or videotape and analyze a lesson. Teams ask, "What can I learn from my colleagues to improve my individual practice?" — [ASCD, "How PLCs Do Data Right"](https://www.ascd.org/el/articles/how-plcs-do-data-right)
- In Schaumburg School District, a data system gives every school access to achievement data from other teachers, teams and schools — [ASCD, "How PLCs Do Data Right" (via search summary)](https://www.ascd.org/el/articles/how-plcs-do-data-right)
- AllThingsPLC describes teams examining results to find strengths and weaknesses in their individual teaching in order to learn from one another — [AllThingsPLC About](https://allthingsplc.info/about/)
- A Solution Tree "From Data Meetings to Data Conversations" protocol (Leiva) starts by reviewing norms, especially data norms, stresses "factual data—no interpretations," and includes deciding who will teach each student group, "including the teacher with highest results" — [Solution Tree handout, Leiva](https://solutiontree.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/solutiontree.com/media/pdf/event-materials/st-charles-plc-23/Leiva_FromDataMeetings_(HO)_36-lorez.pdf)
- A district handbook warns that common assessments "might create teacher anxiety among teachers who recognize that the results might be seen as a judgment of them" — [Mount Vernon CSD PLC Handbook (NYSED)](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/STLE%20PLC%20Handbook%20MVCSD.pdf)
- Disagreements over assessment results can turn meetings into comparison contests. Edutopia recommends reviewing norms every meeting and making sure everyone speaks — [Edutopia, "Overcoming Interpersonal Challenges in PLCs"](https://www.edutopia.org/article/overcoming-interpersonal-challenges-in-plcs)
- Teachers must trust colleagues before they can discuss what is not working. Groups should set and revisit norms — [NCIEA blog](https://www.nciea.org/blog/building-professional-learning-communities/)
- Edutopia: teams use evidence from common assessments and data protocols to determine which strategies were most effective — [Edutopia, "How to Create an Effective PLC"](https://edutopia.org/article/creating-effective-professional-learning-communities)
- EdSurge: teachers review results every two weeks, share successful approaches and trade tactics and resources — [EdSurge, "7 Ways Data-Fueled PLCs Are Transforming Education" (2019)](https://www.edsurge.com/news/2019-10-28-7-ways-data-fueled-plcs-are-transforming-education)
- Teams can also collect data by observing one another teach (live or on video) and tallying how often a practice appears — [Creative Leadership, "PLCs on Steroids"](https://www.creativeleadership.net/resources/plcs-on-steroids/)
- An invitation model of observation: teachers "invite a colleague into their class to observe and provide feedback" on a chosen practice, linked to a PLC inquiry cycle — [Mount Pleasant Primary (Vic.) Coaching & Observation Framework](https://www.mpp.vic.edu.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Coaching-and-Instructional-Observation-Framework.pdf)
- PLC+ learning walks are focused on the team's identified common challenge and are "a way to learn from each other and collaborate on improving instruction without feeling judged" — [Corwin, PLC+ Learning Walks](https://www.corwin.com/docs/default-source/resources-documents/us-1532187-plc-learning-walks.pdf?sfvrsn=96236a58_0); [Fisher et al., *Your Introduction to PLC+*](https://www.corwin.com/docs/default-source/resources-documents/fisher_your-introduction-to-plc-_capacity-building-learning-walks.pdf?sfvrsn=5b7cedfe_1)
- Kentucky's learning walk protocol: a 20–30 minute pre-visit meeting to reset the focus and observation norms, a short visit, then a debrief with notes on a shared tool — [KDE Learning Walk Protocol](https://www.education.ky.gov/curriculum/standards/kyacadstand/Documents/Learning_Walk_Protocol.pdf)

### Inferences
- A non-threatening design is my inference: show results by target or skill first and by teacher second (or only to the team). Frame the prompt as "Which approaches worked for which students?" rather than ranking teachers. Let a teacher whose class did well choose to share a strategy ("Share what I did" or "Invite colleagues to observe"). Pre-load data norms at the start of the data conversation.
- The model of a teacher reteaching a mixed-class group (Leiva) is a concrete, low-drama way to use the strongest results.

### Gaps
- I found no empirical study of how teacher-by-teacher comparison affects trust or outcomes, and no documented opt-in sharing workflow tied directly to CFA results.

## 6. "Start small / get started, then get better": the minimal viable PLC cycle

### Takeaway
Practitioner advice converges on one team, one essential standard and one CFA-to-response cycle, preceded by a quick norms conversation. A first win matters more than a full system.

### Cited Findings
- Solution Tree's "How to start PLC intervention in five steps": choose one team, identify one essential standard, commit to one structured response cycle, and build one win at a time rather than redesigning the whole system — [Solution Tree Blog](https://www.solutiontree.com/blog/formative-assessment-strategies-to-build-plc-interventions/)
- "The first thing the PLC team needs to do is to establish 'norms' and a method to monitor them," then identify essential standards in student-friendly language — [Mount Vernon CSD PLC Handbook](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/STLE%20PLC%20Handbook%20MVCSD.pdf)
- Start with a shared, data-backed problem of practice, because a vague problem produces a vague PLC — [Danielson Group](https://danielsongroup.org/building-community-through-professional-learning/)
- Start meeting weekly, then move to biweekly once next-day moves are routine — [Solved Consulting](https://www.solvedconsulting.com/blog/plc-agenda-templates-30-45-60-minutes-doc)
- Ferriter et al., *The Handbook for Highly Effective Teams in a PLC at Work* (Solution Tree, 2025) offers a step-by-step approach with templates — [Blackwell's listing](https://blackwells.co.uk/bookshop/product/The-Handbook-for-Highly-Effective-Teams-in-a-PLC-at-Work-by-William-M-Ferriter-Bob-Sonju-Anisa-Baker-Busby-Kim-Monkres/9781962188005)

### Inferences
- A minimal viable plan for an app: (1) 5-minute norms, (2) pick one essential standard, (3) write 3–5 CFA items and a proficiency bar, (4) give the CFA and enter results, (5) a 45-minute data meeting that ends with a reteach and extension plan, (6) a one-line "what we'd keep or change" note. Everything else (SMART goals, full curriculum maps) can be added later.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the source of the exact phrase "get started, then get better" (commonly attributed to DuFour/Solution Tree) from the search results.

## 7. What brand-new teachers need to understand PLC vocabulary and expectations

### Takeaway
"PLC" is used loosely and means different things in different schools, so new teachers need a plain-language glossary and a clear statement of what their team actually does, when it meets and what it produces. Solution Tree's glossary is the authoritative vocabulary source.

### Cited Findings
- "PLC" could mean a grade-level team, a content team, the school improvement team, the leadership team or the whole school — [search summary of practitioner guide](https://instructionalstrategies.org/education-acronyms/); see also [Instruction Partners](https://instructionpartners.org/2022/05/23/demystifying-professional-learning/)
- The AllThingsPLC/Solution Tree glossary of PLC terms defines a PLC as "an ongoing process in which educators work collaboratively..." — [AllThingsPLC Terms (PDF)](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf)
- New teachers should not confuse a PLC (shared student-learning goal, regular schedule) with a PLN (a looser network) — [emstudio guide](https://emstudio.pro/blog/curriculum-planning/plc-education-meaning) (low authority)
- Acronym guides for new teachers suggest focusing on the most-used terms in the first year (IEP, 504, MTSS, UDL and so on) rather than memorizing everything — [Instructional Strategies](https://instructionalstrategies.org/education-acronyms/)
- REL Southwest (IES, 2025) published a Facilitator's Guide for PLCs — [IES REL Southwest](https://ies.ed.gov/rel-southwest/2025/01/professional-learning-communities-facilitators-guide) (contents not read)
- *Onboarding Teachers* (SAGE) cites the figure that half of new teachers leave within five years — [SAGE](https://in.sagepub.com/en-in/sas/onboarding-teachers/book285894)

### Inferences
- The app should define terms inline in plain language (essential standard, CFA, learning target, proficiency, intervention, extension, the four critical questions, norms, SMART goal), show an example next to each field, and include a short "what our team meeting will ask of you" orientation.

### Gaps
- I found no research specifically on new teachers' understanding of PLC vocabulary. The evidence is practitioner guidance only.
