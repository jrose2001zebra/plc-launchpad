## 1. The three big ideas and the four critical questions (exact wording; variants; added "fifth/sixth" questions)

### Takeaway
There are three big ideas: a focus on learning, a collaborative culture with collective responsibility, and a results orientation. These turn into four critical questions. The canonical LBD glossary wording is: (1) What is it we want each student to learn? (2) How will we know when each student has learned it? (3) How will we respond when a student experiences difficulty in learning? (4) How will we enrich and extend the learning for students who are proficient? Wording varies slightly from source to source. "Fifth questions" exist in spin-off books, but DuFour deliberately left them out.

### Cited Findings
- **Big idea 1, focus on learning:** "The fundamental purpose of a school is to ensure high levels of learning for all students. This focus on learning translates into four critical questions that drive the daily work of schools." — [AllThingsPLC FAQ](https://allthingsplc.info/faq/)
- **Big idea 2, collaborative culture:** "No school can help all students achieve high levels if teachers work in isolation. Schools improve when teachers are given the time and support to work together to clarify essential student learning, develop common assessments for learning, analyze evidence of student learning, and use that evidence to learn from one another." — [AllThingsPLC FAQ](https://allthingsplc.info/faq/)
- **Big idea 3, results orientation:** "PLCs measure their effectiveness based on results rather than intentions… All staff members receive relevant and timely information on their effectiveness in achieving intended results." — [AllThingsPLC FAQ](https://allthingsplc.info/faq/)
- **FAQ wording of the four questions (each with a sub-question):**
  - "What do we want students to learn? What should each student know and be able to do as a result of each unit, grade level, and/or course?"
  - "How will we know if they have learned? Are we monitoring each student's learning on a timely basis?"
  - "What will we do if they don't learn? What systematic process is in place to provide additional time and support for students who are experiencing difficulty?"
  - "What will we do if they already know what we're teaching?"
  — [AllThingsPLC FAQ](https://allthingsplc.info/faq/)
- **LBD Glossary wording:** "critical questions of collaborative teams. In a PLC, collaboration focuses on four critical questions of learning: (1) What is it we want each student to learn, (2) How will we know when each student has learned, (3) How will we respond when a student experiences difficulty in learning, and (4) How will we enrich and extend the learning for students who are proficient?" — [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf)
- **LBD "Questions to Guide the Work" wording for question 1:** "What is it we want all students to know and be able to do as a result of this course, grade level, or unit of instruction?" — [LBD4 reproducible: Questions to Guide… Clarify Essential Learning](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/questionstoguidetheworkofyourprofessionallearningcommunity_clarifyessentiallearning.pdf)
- **Earlier three-question version:** In 2003, DuFour framed team dialogue around three questions: "What is it we want our students to learn? How will we know when each student has learned it? How can we improve on current levels of student achievement?" — [DuFour, "Collaboration lite," JSD Fall 2003 (Learning Forward)](https://learningforward.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/dufour244.pdf)
- **"Question 2.5" (Kanold/Schuhl math line, adopted in the Plan Book):** In the PDSA reproducible, the team's instructional-sequence planning is labeled "Q2.5: What quality instructional practices will result in high levels of student learning?" — [Plan Book: The PDSA Cycle (Bailey & Jakicic 2019)](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_CTPBPLC/thepdsacycle.pdf)
- **"Fifth question":** *Collaborative Teams That Transform Schools* (Marzano Resources) adds a fifth question, "How will we increase our instructional competence?", and a sixth on schoolwide coordination. An AllThingsPLC blog post, "No Need for a Fifth Question," reports that DuFour left the fifth question out on purpose because schools would overemphasize it and drift back to a focus on teaching rather than learning. — [search result summary: Collaborative Teams that Transform Schools sample](https://issuu.com/grifteducation/docs/3._sample_pages_mrl1587_cttts); [AllThingsPLC blog "No Need for a Fifth Question"](https://allthingsplc.secure.agroup.com/blog/view/369/what-is-a-model-plc-school) (blog content seen only through search snippets; fetching the page returned nothing)
- **Equity variants:** An equity-focused "five PLC questions" set (Fisher et al., 2020) also exists. — search result summary only (no primary source fetched)
- **Wording varies:** DuFour and Reeves noted that "the wording of these questions varies slightly among PLC researchers, the essence of the questions is nearly identical." — ["The futility of PLC Lite" (district copy)](https://shsd55or.gov/media/2019/12/The-futility-of-plc-lite.pdf) (via search snippet)

### Inferences
- For the app, use the LBD Glossary wording as canonical. Show "know and be able to do" as the student-facing gloss of Q1, since the FAQ, the Pathways tool and the Plan Book all use it.
- "What do we want students to be able to do?" is not a separate question. It is the second half of Q1 ("know and be able to do").
- Q2.5 (instructional practices) is a reasonable optional step, but it is not part of the core DuFour model.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the full text of the "No Need for a Fifth Question" blog or verify the exact sixth-question wording in *Collaborative Teams That Transform Schools*.

## 2. Choosing essential (priority/power) standards: criteria, process, output chart

### Takeaway
Teams use Reeves' and Ainsworth's tests: endurance, leverage, readiness for the next level, and (in LBD) likelihood of being assessed on high-stakes tests. Teams apply them in a consensus protocol: individual review, then consensus standard by standard, then checks against student data, test blueprints and vertical alignment. The output is an Essential Standards Chart listing each standard with its proficiency example, prerequisites, timing, assessment and extension.

### Cited Findings
- **LBD four-part test:** "What knowledge and which skills in our curriculum pass the four-part test: (a) endurance, (b) leverage, (c) necessity for success at the next level, and (d) likelihood to be assessed on high-stakes external tests?" The same tool also asks "What material can we eliminate from our curriculum?" and "How should we pace the curriculum…?" — [LBD4 Questions to Guide… Clarify Essential Learning](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/questionstoguidetheworkofyourprofessionallearningcommunity_clarifyessentiallearning.pdf)
- **Glossary definitions (Reeves, 2002):**
  - Endurance is "knowledge, skills, and dispositions students are expected to retain over time as opposed to those they merely learn for a test."
  - Curriculum leverage is "skills, knowledge, and dispositions that will assist the student in becoming proficient in other areas of the curriculum and other academic disciplines."
  - Readiness is "having acquired the skills, knowledge, and dispositions essential for success in the next unit, course, or grade level."
  - Essential learning "may also be referred to as essential outcomes, essential standards, power standards…, guaranteed and viable curriculum."
  — [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf)
- **RTI at Work criteria tool:** Endurance asks whether the standard is "valuable beyond a single test date"; leverage whether it is "valuable in multiple disciplines"; readiness whether it is "essential for success in the next grade or level". Framing question: "Of all that we teach and cover, what standards are so essential that all students must learn and master them?" — [RTI at Work Plan Book: Essential Standards Selection Criteria](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_RTIAWPB/essentialstandardsselectioncriteria.pdf)
- **R.E.A.L. acronym:** Readiness, Endurance, Assessed, Leverage, created by Ted Horrell and colleagues in Tennessee from Ainsworth's (2004) *Power Standards*. One protocol suggests choosing about one-third of standards as essential. — [search summaries: Idaho SDE "ES Article Real Criteria"](https://www.sde.idaho.gov/academic/standards/files/toolkit/ES-Article-Real-Criteria.pdf); [AllThingsPLC essential standard protocols 2020](https://allthingsplc.secure.agroup.com/files/uploads/essential-standard-protocols-for-identification-spring-2020.pdf) (this URL returned 404 when fetched; claim taken from the search snippet)
- **Identifying Essential Standards Protocol, 7 steps with times** (adapted from Ainsworth 2004):
  1. Discuss the criteria (5 min): "All essential standards should have endurance, leverage, and readiness and should be needed for high-stakes assessments."
  2. Decide how to organize the standards for review, e.g., by strand (5 min).
  3. Each member independently picks standards that fit the criteria (5–10 min per strand).
  4. Build consensus "one at a time" (30+ min).
  5. Examine student performance data and add standards to shore up weaknesses (20 min).
  6. Check district or state documents, test specifications and assessment blueprints (20 min).
  7. Vertically align with the grade levels or courses above and below (60 min, after all teams have drafts).
  — [Plan Book: Identifying Essential Standards Protocol](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_CTPBPLC/identifyingessentialstandardsprotocol.pdf)
- **Essential Standards Chart output fields** ("What Is It We Expect Students to Learn?"):
  - Header: Grade, Subject, Semester, Team Members.
  - Columns: **Description of Standard** ("What is the essential standard to be learned? Describe in student-friendly vocabulary."); **Example of Rigor** ("What does proficient student work look like? Provide an example or description."); **Prerequisite Skills** ("What prior knowledge, skills, or vocabulary are needed…?"); **When Taught?**; **Common End-of-Unit Assessment** ("What assessments will be used to measure student mastery?"); **Extension Standards** ("What will we do when students have already learned this standard?").
  — [RTI at Work Plan Book: Essential Standards Chart](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_RTIAWPB/rtiatworkessentialstandardschart.pdf) (adapted from Buffum, Mattos & Weber 2012). A *Taking Action* 2nd-ed. version is listed at [TARTI2nd index](https://www.solutiontree.com/free-resources/rti/tarti2nd).
- **Products for Q1 (How to Leverage PLCs):**
  - "List of essential grade-level or content-specific standards" (using endurance, leverage, readiness)
  - "Aligned curriculum guides" (vertical alignment)
  - "Pacing guide or course sequence"
  — [PLC Products, Tasks, and Time Lines](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_HLPLCSI/plcproductstasksandtimelines.pdf)
- **Critical Issues items 3–6:**
  - Item 3: each member is clear on essential learning for the course and each unit.
  - Item 4: essential learning is aligned with state standards and high-stakes tests.
  - Item 5: "identified course content and topics we can eliminate."
  - Item 6: agreed sequence and "pacing guides."
  — [LBD4: Critical Issues for Team Consideration](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/criticalissuesforteamconsideration.pdf)
- **Pathways Tool, Q1 "Prioritizing Standards" questions:** "Which standards provide endurance? …readiness…? …leverage? …are most often assessed by standardized tests? If you could only teach ten standards in this course, which would they be? Why?" — [LBD4: Pathways Tool for Critical Question One (from *Amplify Your Impact*, 2018)](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/pathwaystoolforcriticalquestionone.pdf)
- **Guaranteed and viable curriculum:** a curriculum that "(1) gives students in the same course or grade level access to the same essential learning regardless of who is teaching the class and (2) can be taught in the time allotted (Marzano, 2003)." — [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf)

### Inferences
- Essential standards are normally chosen once a year ("Big picture look for the year" in the PDSA tool), not per unit. A unit plan in the app should pull from an already-agreed list. The app could include a one-time "Year setup: essential standards chart" step.
- The minimum viable chart row for the app: standard (student-friendly), proficiency example, prerequisites, when taught (unit), end-of-unit assessment, extension.

### Gaps
- I found no official fixed number or percentage of essential standards in the DuFour texts. The "about one-third" figure comes from a district or AllThingsPLC protocol seen only as a search snippet.

## 3. Unwrapping/unpacking a standard into learning targets; target types; "I can" language

### Takeaway
Teams unwrap a standard in four moves: circle the verbs (skills), underline the nouns (concepts), bracket the context or stimulus, then list targets with a DOK level and vocabulary. Bailey and Jakicic's Plan Book sorts targets into a simple "know / do" pair. The RTI at Work Plan Book uses the Stiggins four-way split: knowledge, reasoning, performance skills, product. The LBD glossary and several Solution Tree reproducibles explicitly recommend student-friendly "I can" statements.

### Cited Findings
- **Learning target definition:** "Subsets of a standard, and typically, there are multiple learning targets within a single essential standard… learning targets represent the individual concepts and skills embedded within each standard" (Mattos et al., 2016, pp. 75–76). — [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf)
- **"I can" statements:** "Learning targets translated into student-friendly language and usually arranged in a checklist format by teacher teams. 'I can' statements are used to clarify essential learning expectations and enable students to assume responsibility for and monitor their own work." — [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf)
- **Unwrapping Standards Protocol, 5 steps:**
  1. Clarify which essential standard to unwrap for the upcoming unit.
  2. Annotate it: "Circle the verbs… Underline the significant nouns or noun phrases… Bracket any information that describes the context or stimulus."
  3. Record the targets in a graphic organizer and "read between the lines": "What are the smaller steps of learning that will lead to students' learning this standard?"
  4. Set the rigor of each target using Webb's DOK (1 recall, 2 skills/concepts, 3 strategic thinking, 4 extended thinking).
  5. Identify academic vocabulary.
  — [Plan Book: Unwrapping Standards Protocol](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_CTPBPLC/unwrappingstandardsprotocol.pdf)
- **Unwrapping Standards Template:** Columns are Guiding Question, Learning Targets, Depth of Knowledge Level, Assessment Ideas. Rows (guiding questions) are:
  - "What concepts, big ideas, definitions, or information will students need to know?"
  - "What smaller skills or subskills will students need to do?"
  - "What academic language or vocabulary…?"
  - "What context or stimulus (text, problem type, or situation) will students encounter?"
  — [Plan Book: Unwrapping Standards Template](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_CTPBPLC/unwrappingstandardstemplate.pdf)
- **Stiggins four types:** The RTI at Work "Essential Standards Unit Plan" has checkboxes for Knowledge, Reasoning, Performance skills and Product, and columns for "Knowledge Targets / Reasoning Targets / Performance Skills / Product Targets". It then has a "Student-friendly learning targets" field and an assessment table (Assessment, Connection to Standard, Student Involvement, Time Line). Header fields: Essential standard, End-of-unit assessment, When taught, Instructional days needed. — [RTI at Work Plan Book: Essential Standards Unit Plan](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_RTIAWPB/essentialstandardsunitplan.pdf)
- **Origin of the four types:** Knowledge, reasoning, skill and product come from Stiggins, Arter, Chappuis and Chappuis (some versions add dispositions). Reasoning verbs include "analyze, compare-contrast, synthesize, classify, infer-deduce, evaluate". Passive "I can understand" targets are discouraged because students cannot demonstrate them. — search result summaries ([Kentucky DOE Learning Targets](https://www.education.ky.gov/school/stratclsgap/currandstand/Documents/Learning%20Targets.pdf); [OCALI Deconstructing Standards](https://ocali.org/storage/ocali-ims-sites/ocali-ims-ocali/documents/Deconstructing_Standards.pdf)). These are not PLC at Work primary sources.
- **TEAMS deconstruction template (*Literacy in a PLC at Work*, 2023), 10 steps:**
  1. Label the unit and the standard.
  2. Look at the standards in the grade below and above, plus prerequisites.
  3. Read notes in local standards and curriculum guides.
  4. Use assessment blueprints and released items.
  5. Pull nouns and content.
  6. Pull verbs and skills.
  7. Write sentence frames for academic vocabulary.
  8. "Use nouns and verbs to create student-friendly I can statements arranged in a learning progression from simple to complex."
  9. Write guiding questions.
  10. Group "I can" statements into learning goals.
  — [LPLC: TEAMS Process Template: Deconstructing Essential Standards](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LPLC/TEAMSprocesstemplatedeconstructingessentialstandards.pdf)
- **Taking Action Unit Assessment Plan:**
  - Step 1: "deconstruct each essential standard to identify the learning targets."
  - Step 2: "Organize the learning targets into a sequenced learning progression… List skills and concepts from least complex to most complex," with "Student-Friendly Targets."
  — [Taking Action: Unit Assessment Plan](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_TARTI2nd/unitassessmentplan.pdf)
- **Student-friendly targets and anchor papers as products:** "Write learning targets in student-friendly language by engaging students in the process" produces "Student-friendly targets." "Create and share anchor papers with students demonstrating strong and weak work" produces "Anchor papers." — [PLC Products, Tasks, and Time Lines](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_HLPLCSI/plcproductstasksandtimelines.pdf)
- **Pathways Q1, "Identifying Targets / Determining Proficiency":** "What targets did the unwrapping process reveal?… How would you rewrite this target in student-friendly terms?… What are the prerequisite skills and vocabulary…? To what DOK level should students show mastery? What will students create, produce, or be able to do when they master this target?" — [LBD4 Pathways Tool Q1](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/pathwaystoolforcriticalquestionone.pdf)
- **"Before" phase:** Teams ask "What are the concepts, information, definitions, and processes that students will know? What are the skills, performances, and actions that students will be able to do or demonstrate?" and "reach consensus around a [picture of proficiency]… cognitive demand." — [Solution Tree Blog: The Three Critical Phases of Common Assessment Practice](https://www.solutiontree.com/blog/?p=4874)

### Inferences
- The app's target step should capture, for each target: target text (teacher language), an "I can" version, type (know/do as the default; optionally knowledge/reasoning/skill/product), DOK level, vocabulary, and context or stimulus. Ordering targets simple to complex supports the learning progression.
- Solution Tree authors clearly endorse "I can" language. It appears in the LBD glossary, the TEAMS template, the RTI at Work chart ("Describe in student-friendly vocabulary") and *How to Leverage PLCs*.

### Gaps
- I found no single DuFour-authored rule for when to use know/do versus the four Stiggins types. Different Solution Tree authors use different schemes.

## 4. Defining proficiency: what proficient work looks like, scoring agreements, rubrics, calibration

### Takeaway
Before teaching or assessing, teams must agree on a picture of proficiency for each target. That means a cut score (e.g., "two out of three correct," or "level 3 on the rubric"), a rubric or scoring guide, and an example of proficient work. Teams also calibrate their scoring (inter-rater reliability) so that "common" data really are common.

### Cited Findings
- **Performance-based assessment requirement:** Before giving a common performance-based assessment, a team "must (1) agree on the criteria by which members will judge the quality of student work and (2) demonstrate that they apply those criteria consistently—establishing inter-rater reliability." — [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf)
- **LBD questions:** "Have we agreed on what proficient student work looks like? Can we consistently apply our agreed-on criteria for student work to ensure students receive reliable feedback?" — [LBD4 Questions to Guide… Clarify Essential Learning](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/questionstoguidetheworkofyourprofessionallearningcommunity_clarifyessentiallearning.pdf)
- **Critical Issues items 11, 14, 15 and 17:**
  - Item 11: "established the proficiency standard we want each student to achieve on each skill and concept examined with our common assessments."
  - Item 14: "agreed on the criteria we will use in judging the quality of student work… and we continually practice applying those criteria to ensure we are consistent."
  - Item 15: "taught students the criteria… and provided them with examples."
  - Item 17: a proficiency standard for summative assessments.
  — [LBD4 Critical Issues for Team Consideration](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/criticalissuesforteamconsideration.pdf)
- **Example cut scores:** "Review the expectations for proficiency (for example, two out of three correct on a multiple-choice assessment, or a level 3 on the rubric)." — [Figure 5.6 Protocol for Using CFA Data (Bailey & Jakicic 2017, p. 82)](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LGRWE/figure5.6protocolforusingcommonformativeassessmentdata.pdf)
- **Proficiency products (Q2):** "Determine proficiency levels" produces a "Rationale." "Develop grading rubric or scoring guide" produces a "Rubric or written criteria." — [PLC Products, Tasks, and Time Lines](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_HLPLCSI/plcproductstasksandtimelines.pdf)
- **Agree before giving the assessment:** "Reaching agreement about proficiency on the assessment up front, in other words, [before] the assessment is given, is critical." — [Solution Tree Blog: Three Critical Phases](https://www.solutiontree.com/blog/?p=4874)
- **Pathways Q1, "Determining Proficiency":** "How will you grade or score this target? What models of proficiency do you have or can you create?" Pathways Q2, "Analyzing Student Work": "Find examples of student work you deem proficient. What makes them proficient? Do you all agree?" — [Pathways Q1](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/pathwaystoolforcriticalquestionone.pdf); [Pathways Q2](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/pathwaysforcriticalquestiontwo.pdf)
- **Scoring Calibration Protocol** (*Handbook for Collaborative Common Assessments*, 2019):
  - Decide the time available, the sample size and the item or criterion to review.
  - "Teachers should not identify their own student work for review"; pick low, medium and high samples from each other's piles; hide names.
  - Each member scores on a hidden sticky note and passes the paper around the table.
  - Sort papers into stacks by score plus a "mixed-number stack."
  - "Go directly to the mixed-number stack to engage in a calibration discussion… Come to consensus." After that, teachers score the rest of their own work.
  — [HCCA: Protocols for Examining Evidence and Artifacts](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_HCCA/protocolsforexaminingevidenceandartifacts.pdf)
- **"Common" includes common criteria:** A common assessment uses "the same criteria for assessing the quality of student work." — [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf)

### Inferences
- App fields for each target: proficiency criterion (cut score or rubric level), a short description of proficient work, an optional exemplar or anchor upload, and a "calibrated? (date)" checkbox.

### Gaps
- I found no official template that fixes a standard rubric scale. The 4-point scale appears in examples ("level 3") but is not required.

## 5. Common formative assessment (CFA): definition, length, timing, design steps, number per unit, pre-assessment

### Takeaway
A CFA is a short, team-made, target-by-target assessment. All teachers give it within a narrow window, and it is scored against agreed criteria so the team can respond quickly. Teams assess only a few targets, chosen because they are hard, prerequisite or essential. Bailey and Jakicic's design protocol has 7 steps. Units typically include several CFAs plus a common end-of-unit (summative) assessment. External research cited in LBD recommends assessing "at least every three to six weeks." Pre-assessment of prerequisite skills appears in the Critical Issues checklist, but I found no dedicated DuFour pre-test template.

### Cited Findings
- **CFA definition:** "An assessment typically created collaboratively by a team of teachers responsible for the same grade level or course. Common formative assessments are used frequently throughout the year to identify:
  1. individual students who need additional time and support for learning,
  2. highly proficient students who would benefit from extension,
  3. the teaching strategies most effective in helping students acquire the intended knowledge and skills,
  4. curriculum and professional development concerns…, and
  5. improvement goals for individual teachers and the team."
  — [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf)
- **Common assessment definition:** Given "at the same time or within a very narrow window of time and use the same criteria… Team-developed common formative assessments are the lynchpin of the PLC process. They provide team members with the basis of comparison that turns data into information." — [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf)
- **Length:** "The assessment needs to be short and easy enough to score so that the team can respond quickly to the results." Also: "Remember you do not have to assess every learning target." — [Plan Book: Developing Assessments Protocol](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_CTPBPLC/developingassessmentsprotocol.pdf); same text in [CFA 2nd ed. Figure 5.7 (Bailey & Jakicic 2012, p. 107)](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_CFA2/figure57sampleprotocolfordevelopinganassessment.pdf)
- **7-step design process:**
  1. **Decide what to assess.** "Which targets are most likely to cause certain students difficulty? Which targets are most important or are prerequisite skills…? Which targets are absolutely necessary…?"
  2. **Decide how to assess.** Agree on the thinking level for each target; choose "selected-response, constructed-response, or performance assessment."
  3. **Develop the assessment plan.** Use a planning chart or blueprint; "what type of items and how many items… on each target. Consider how long the assessment will take to administer and… to score."
  4. **Determine the timeline.** Set administration dates and the data meeting date, allowing for scoring time.
  5. **Write the assessment** using quality item-writing guidelines.
  6. **Review the assessment** before giving it (clear directions).
  7. **Set proficiency criteria** and decide how data will be "reported back by learning target and by student."
  — [Developing Assessments Protocol](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_CTPBPLC/developingassessmentsprotocol.pdf)
- **Five-Step Unit-Planning Template, Step 4:** "Identify specific targets the team will commonly assess formatively. Team members should collectively monitor learning targets that are typically challenging… Identify or develop brief but aligned assessment items… discuss the level of proficiency." — [Plan Book: Five-Step Unit-Planning Template](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_CTPBPLC/five-stepunit-planningtemplate.pdf)
- **ACID test for evaluating a CFA:**
  - **A**ligned (context, content, rigor/DOK; is the mastery level appropriate?)
  - **C**lear (items clearly written)
  - **I**nformative (meaningful data that steer interventions; a chance for feedback)
  - **D**esigned to reflect the demands of the standards (similar to high-stakes tests, reasoning, evidence)
  — [HHET: Using the ACID Test (Ferriter 2020, adapted from Bailey & Jakicic 2017)](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_HHET/toolusingtheacidtesttoevaluateacommonformativeassessment.pdf)
- **Pathways Q2, "Creating CFAs":**
  - "When will you deliver your next CFA? What targets will you address…? What targets from previous instruction do you need to reassess?"
  - DOK match; question types.
  - "Are there enough items per target…? Do your multiple-choice items include distractors that will help you identify specific misconceptions? When will you analyze the CFA data as a team?"
  — [LBD4 Pathways for Critical Question Two](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/pathwaysforcriticalquestiontwo.pdf)
- **Frequency evidence quoted in LBD:** "High-growth schools and districts use frequent, common short-cycle assessments—at least every three to six weeks. Teachers create formative assessments before developing their lessons for a unit and clarify success criteria" (Battelle for Kids, 2013). — [LBD4: Why Should We Use Common Assessments?](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/whyshouldweusecommonassessments.pdf)
- **Number per unit:**
  - RTI at Work tools assume a mid-unit common assessment ("Mid-Unit Common Assessment Protocol," "Team Response for Mid-Unit Preventions") plus an end-of-unit common assessment ("End-of-Unit Common Assessments Protocol to Target Tier 2 Interventions"). — [RTI at Work Plan Book reproducibles index](https://solutiontree.com/free-resources/rtiatwork/rtiawpb)
  - The Taking Action Unit Assessment Plan has columns for "End-of-Unit Assessment" and "Targets Assessed on a Common Formative Assessment" and asks "How many questions will there be for each target." — [Taking Action: Unit Assessment Plan](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_TARTI2nd/unitassessmentplan.pdf)
  - The third-grade SMART goal example schedules common formative writing prompts at "midpoint of each grading period." — [LBD4 SMART Goal Worksheet: Third-Grade Team](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/SMARTgoalworksheetthirdgradeteam.pdf)
- **Pre-assessment / prerequisites (Critical Issues items 7–9):**
  - "identified the prerequisite knowledge and skills students need in order to master the essential learning of each unit"
  - "identified strategies and created instruments to assess whether students have the prerequisite knowledge and skills"
  - "developed strategies and systems to assist students in acquiring prerequisite knowledge and skills when they are lacking"
  — [LBD4 Critical Issues for Team Consideration](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/criticalissuesforteamconsideration.pdf)
- **Products for Q2:**
  - "Assessment map for each essential standard" (from mapping summative and formative assessments to each standard)
  - "Develop common formative assessments for each learning target" produces "Common formative assessments"
  — [PLC Products, Tasks, and Time Lines](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_HLPLCSI/plcproductstasksandtimelines.pdf)
- **Not every assessment is common:** "Not every assessment should be a common assessment. There is still a place for individual teachers to create their own formal assessments." — [DuFour, DuFour & Eaker, "The Case for Common Formative Assessments," AllThingsPLC](https://allthingsplc.info/the-case-for-common-formative-assessments/)

### Inferences
- An app "complete CFA" record needs: targets assessed (a subset), item type and number of items per target, proficiency cut per target, administration window, scoring deadline, data-meeting date, an ACID check, and a reassessment plan.
- One reasonable default per unit: an optional pre-assessment of prerequisites, 1–2 short CFAs (mid-unit), and one common end-of-unit assessment. This is an inference from the tools above, not a fixed rule.

### Gaps
- I found no DuFour-specified CFA length in minutes or items. The guidance is qualitative ("short," "brief," "enough items per target"). Some district summaries suggest roughly 3–4 items per target, but I did not verify this in a Solution Tree primary source.

## 6. SMART goals: team-level vs. unit-level, template fields, example wording

### Takeaway
SMART stands for Strategic and Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Results-oriented, and Time-bound (O'Neill and Conzemius). The LBD SMART Goal Worksheet ties a team goal to district and school goals and lists strategies, owners, timeline and evidence. The canonical LBD example is an annual team goal ("Last year 85%… This year at least 90%…"). Bailey and Jakicic's PDSA cycle and the TEAMS template also use SMART goals for end-of-unit assessments, so both levels exist.

### Cited Findings
- **Definition:** SMART goals are "Goals that are Strategic and Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Results-oriented, and Time-bound (O'Neill & Conzemius, 2005)." Attainable goals are "intended to document incremental progress and build momentum and self-efficacy through short-term wins." — [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf)
- **LBD SMART Goal Worksheet fields:**
  - Header: School, Team Name, Team Leader, Team Members, District Goals, School Goals.
  - Columns: Team SMART Goal, Strategies and Action Steps, Who Is Responsible, Target Date or Timeline, Evidence of Effectiveness.
  — [LBD4: SMART Goal Worksheet](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/SMARTgoalworksheet.pdf)
- **Canonical example (Third-Grade Team):**
  - "Our Current Reality: Last year, 85 percent of our students met or exceeded the target score of 3 on our state's writing prompt in May. Our SMART Goal: This year, at least 90 percent of our students will meet or exceed the target score of 3 on our state's writing prompt in May."
  - Action steps include clarifying and pacing essential outcomes (by October 15), collaboratively scored common formative writing prompts, intervention and enrichment sessions, parent workshops, and a team action research project.
  - Evidence is listed as e.g. "Common writing rubric," "Intervention/enrichment schedule."
  — [LBD4: SMART Goal Worksheet: Third-Grade Team](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/SMARTgoalworksheetthirdgradeteam.pdf)
- **Plan Book SMART Goal Template columns:**
  - "Action Steps and Products (What steps or activities will you initiate…? What products will you create?)"
  - "Team Members (Who is responsible…?)"
  - "Time Frame"
  - "Results and Evaluation (How will you assess your progress? What evidence…?)"
  - Plus: "Identify a student achievement SMART goal."
  — [Plan Book: SMART Goal Template (adapted from DuFour, DuFour & Eaker 2006 PLC Plan Book; Kanold & Schuhl 2020)](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_CTPBPLC/smartgoaltemplate.pdf)
- **Unit-level SMART goals:** In the PDSA "Plan" phase for each unit, teams design "Summative and formative assessment items and timing (Q2…), using SMART… goals for end-of-unit assessments." — [Plan Book: The PDSA Cycle](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_CTPBPLC/thepdsacycle.pdf)
- **TEAMS analysis template step 9:** "Aim to achieve goals: Did we meet our collective goal? By what margin? What data do we need to collect to continue to monitor our SMART goal attainment? How do these data impact upcoming unit goals?" — [LPLC: TEAMS Analyzing Data template](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LPLC/TEAMSprocesstemplateanalyzingdataforevidenceofinstructionaleffectiveness.pdf)
- **Critical Issues item 2:** "We have analyzed student achievement data and established SMART goals to improve on this level of achievement we are working interdependently to attain." — [LBD4 Critical Issues](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/criticalissuesforteamconsideration.pdf)
- **Inputs vs. outputs:** DuFour says teams should shift the focus "from teacher inputs (for example, whether teachers accomplished their goal of creating a new unit…) to student outcomes." — [DuFour, JSD 2003](https://learningforward.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/dufour244.pdf)
- **Products:** "Establish team SMART goals" produces "SMART goals." "Continuously monitor progress…" produces "SMART goal action plan." — [PLC Products, Tasks, and Time Lines](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_HLPLCSI/plcproductstasksandtimelines.pdf)

### Inferences
- App template for a unit goal: "Current reality: __% of our students were proficient on [standard/assessment] (last year or pre-assessment). SMART goal: By [date / end of unit], at least __% of our students will score [proficiency level] or higher on our common [end-of-unit assessment] on [essential standard(s)]." This mirrors the LBD example, but at unit scale.
- SMART goals must be student-learning results, not teacher tasks.

### Gaps
- I found no official LBD unit-level SMART goal example. The unit-level use comes from Bailey and Jakicic and the TEAMS literacy tools.

## 7. Data analysis after a CFA: protocols, questions, and teacher-by-teacher comparison

### Takeaway
Teams meet as soon as possible after a CFA. They look at results one target at a time and student by student. They group students by specific error or misconception and compare results across classrooms to find the most effective instructional strategies. Then they plan reteaching or extension and write reassessment items. DuFour argues that comparing teachers is the point: a "basis of comparison" turns data into information, so teachers can learn from colleagues whose students did better.

### Cited Findings
- **Analyzing CFA Data Protocol (Plan Book, adapted from Bailey & Jakicic 2017):** Meet "as soon as possible"; analyze "each individually assessed learning target" rather than total points. Steps:
  1. Set the stage: purpose, data norms (2 min).
  2. Review the assessment's focus: targets, items, proficiency expectations, scoring questions (2 min).
  3. Discuss data and find patterns: "Look at overall results… by learning target… identify specific students… regroup them by specific need (for example, students who made a calculation error versus students who chose the wrong solution pathway)" (10 min, "one essential learning target at a time").
  4. Determine instructional responses: small-group reteach vs. whole-class re-engagement; "Have each teacher share his or her original instructional strategy so that the team can see if one strategy worked better"; a plan for each misconception; consult best practice or coaches (10–15 min; "This is your action plan and should include timelines").
  5. Identify or develop reassessment items: "the reassessment may be done orally or may be a version of the original assessment" (5–10 min).
  6. Discuss learnings for the team's future work: adjust curriculum, pacing, items; effective and ineffective strategies (2–5 min).
  — [Plan Book: Analyzing CFA Data Protocol](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_CTPBPLC/analyzingcommonformativeassessmentdataprotocol.pdf); earlier version: [Figure 5.6 (Bailey & Jakicic 2017 p. 82)](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LGRWE/figure5.6protocolforusingcommonformativeassessmentdata.pdf)
- **Team Protocol for Analyzing Common Assessment Data** (*Taking Action*, from Buffum, Mattos & Weber 2012). Fits a 45–60 min meeting if teachers review their own data first. Questions:
  1. "Which students did not demonstrate mastery on which specific standards? Which students mastered… and need extension? (by the student, by the standard, and by the target)"
  2. "Which learning targets require more of our attention as a team?"
  3. "Which instructional practices proved to be most effective? How do we know?"
  4. "What patterns can we identify from students' errors?…misconceptions?"
  5. "What instruction and intervention are needed…?"
  6. "How will we extend learning…?"
  7. "How can we improve this assessment?"
  — [Taking Action: Team Protocol for Analyzing Common Assessment Data](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_TARTI2nd/teamprotocolforanalyzingcommonassessmentdata.pdf)
- **Mid-Unit Common Assessment Protocol:**
  - Consider the task (what worked, what didn't, revisions).
  - Analyze: "which learning targets require more attention?… which students did not master which targets?… which classroom or classrooms require additional support?… As an individual teacher, which area was my lowest, and how can I improve?"
  - Create a team plan of action covering assessment, curricular and instructional changes.
  — [RTI at Work Plan Book: Mid-Unit Common Assessment Protocol](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_RTIAWPB/midunitcommonassessmentprotocol.pdf)
- **AllThingsPLC "Data Protocol"** (Friziellie, Schmidt & Spiller 2018). It frames the four questions, then:
  1. "Based on the data, how does data differ classroom to classroom? (Here's what…)"
  2. "What are the implications…? Which instructional strategies helped students learn? What skills did the proficient students demonstrate…? (So what?)"
  3. "So what's the plan? (Now what? Who? What? When?)"
  — [AllThingsPLC: Data Protocol](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/DataProtocol.pdf)
- **TEAMS "Analyzing Data for Evidence of Instructional Effectiveness" (10 steps):**
  1. Name the unit assessment.
  2. Celebrate student strengths.
  3. Discover student opportunities.
  4. Organize support by student and by target (Level 1 Emerging / 2 Progressing / 3 Attaining).
  5. Plan actions to accelerate or extend.
  6. Claim team strengths, including "Individual Teacher Strengths."
  7. Target team need, including "Individual Teacher Needs."
  8. Learn: the professional learning needed (team and individual).
  9. Aim: did we meet our goal, and by what margin?
  10. Commit to next steps (team and individual commitments).
  — [LPLC TEAMS template](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LPLC/TEAMSprocesstemplateanalyzingdataforevidenceofinstructionaleffectiveness.pdf)
- **Pathways Q2, "Analyzing Assessment Data":** "What are the proficiency rates of each target in individual classrooms?… overall team proficiency rates…? Are proficiency levels higher in some classes than in others? Why? How can you transfer that success to other classes? Which questions did the students most often get wrong?… patterns among the wrong answers?" — [LBD4 Pathways Q2](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/pathwaysforcriticalquestiontwo.pdf)
- **Why teacher-by-teacher comparison:** "Most educators can teach an entire career and not know if they teach a particular concept more or less effectively than the teacher next door because the assessments they generate for their isolated classrooms never provide them with a basis of comparison." In a PLC, every teacher gets feedback on their students' proficiency "in comparison to other students attempting to achieve the same standard." And: "When teachers are presented with clear evidence their students are not becoming proficient… and that similar students taught by their colleagues have demonstrated proficiency on the same assessment, they are open to exploring new practices… we consider team-developed common formative assessments one of the most powerful motivators." — [DuFour, DuFour & Eaker, "The Case for Common Formative Assessments"](https://allthingsplc.info/the-case-for-common-formative-assessments/)
- **Data vs. information:** "To transform data into information requires putting data in context, and this typically requires a basis of comparison." DRIP syndrome means "data rich/information poor." — [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf). DuFour (2003): "If… each teacher has access only to the results of his or her students, without any comparison to other students in the school, team members will not be able to ascertain the strengths and weaknesses of their individual instruction." — [JSD 2003](https://learningforward.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/dufour244.pdf)
- **Bring student work:** "Ideally, teams bring actual student work to the table so members can visually examine strengths, error patterns, and potential misconceptions." — [Solution Tree Blog: Three Critical Phases](https://www.solutiontree.com/blog/?p=4874)
- **Products:** "Analyze assessment results" produces "Item analysis, data-team protocol, or both." "Identify students for interventions by essential standard or learning target and specific need" produces "List of students with specific data." — [PLC Products, Tasks, and Time Lines](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_HLPLCSI/plcproductstasksandtimelines.pdf)
- **"A Data Picture of Our School":** an LBD4 reproducible used at the school level (current-reality data), not for unit CFA analysis. — [LBD4 index](https://www.solutiontree.com/free-resources/plcbooks/lbd4) (title only; contents not read)

### Inferences
- App data step views: (a) target x student grid (proficient / not yet / extend); (b) target x teacher proficiency rates; (c) error and misconception groups; (d) strategy notes per teacher. The Essential Standards Student-Tracking Chart (Student Name x Standard x Targets 1–4) is the official model for view (a). — [Plan Book: Essential Standards Student-Tracking Chart](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_CTPBPLC/essentialstandardsstudent-trackingchart.pdf)

## 8. Responses: Tier 2 intervention and extension, planning, timing, re-assessment

### Takeaway
Teams plan responses by target and by specific need. Students fall into three groups: "Need Additional Time and Support / On Target / Need Extension." Teams decide on small-group reteaching or whole-class re-engagement, provide the help during the school day in schoolwide flexible (Tier 2) time without pulling students from new core instruction, and reassess with new items to confirm learning. Extension means going deeper on essential standards. It is distinct from "enrichment."

### Cited Findings
- **Team Response for Mid-Unit Preventions:** For each essential standard there are three columns: "Need Additional Time and Support / On Target / Need Extension". Each column lists "Students:" and an "Instructional plan:". — [RTI at Work Plan Book: Team Response for Mid-Unit Preventions](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_RTIAWPB/teamresponseformidunitpreventions.pdf)
- **Formative use in a PLC:** Teams use CFAs to "(1) identify students who are experiencing difficulty… (2) provide those students with additional time and support in a way that does not remove them from new direct instruction, (3) give them additional opportunities to demonstrate their learning, and (4) inform and improve the individual and collective instructional practices of the team." — [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf)
- **Systematic intervention:** "A schoolwide plan that ensures every student… will receive additional time and support for learning as soon as he or she experiences difficulty… occurs during the school day, and students are required rather than invited… what happens when a student does not learn is no longer left to the individual teacher to determine." Intervention is "anything a school does, above and beyond what all students receive" (Buffum, Mattos & Weber, 2012). — [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf)
- **Extension vs. enrichment:** "**extension.** When students are stretched beyond essential grade-level curriculum or levels of proficiency… demonstrate mastery of essential standards at a level beyond what is deemed grade-level proficient, providing… more of the required grade-level curriculum that is deemed nonessential, or… curriculum above their current grade level." Enrichment refers to specials and electives and activities like field trips. — [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf)
- **Flexible / Tier 2 time questions:** "How will you successfully use this time to support and extend student learning? How will you determine which interventions to offer?… which staff member should lead each intervention? How will students transition…? How will the school hold students accountable?… monitor student progress efficiently? How will this time serve students who don't need extra help?… who need help in multiple academic areas?… avoid a process that becomes a paperwork nightmare?… use only current resources…?" — [RTI at Work Plan Book: Effective Use of Tier 2 Time](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_RTIAWPB/effectiveuseoftier2time.pdf). *Taking Action* 2nd ed. also lists "Using Flexible Time Well" and a "Creating Flexible Time Worksheet" under the guiding coalition (schoolwide), not individual teams. — [TARTI2nd index (search summary)](https://solutiontree.com/free-resources/rti/tarti2nd)
- **Reassessment:** Step 5 of the analysis protocol is to "Identify or develop the items… to monitor whether students met the learning target after receiving support… may be done orally or may be a version of the original assessment." — [Plan Book: Analyzing CFA Data Protocol](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_CTPBPLC/analyzingcommonformativeassessmentdataprotocol.pdf). The PDSA "Act" phase: "Teams act on their game plan to support and gather additional evidence of student learning to ensure that their support resulted in higher levels of student learning." — [Plan Book: PDSA Cycle](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_CTPBPLC/thepdsacycle.pdf)
- **Pathways Q3 ("Planning Classroom Interventions," "Utilizing a System of Supports"):** "How can you divide students into groups based on need?… adjustments in the content (lower Lexile materials, pictorial explanations…)?… a different process… (peer tutoring, cooperative learning…)?… Can students create a different kind of product…? Has this student been identified to receive Tier 2 or Tier 3 support?" — [LBD4 Pathways Q3](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/pathwaysforcriticalquestionthree.pdf)
- **Pathway Q4 (extension):** "What DOK level constitutes proficiency? At what DOK level did your students perform?… What pieces of the content could you help your students stretch even further?… higher-Lexile-level materials… writing to learn, advance organizers… What different kinds of products (with a higher DOK) can students create…?" — [LBD4 Pathway for Critical Question Four](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/pathwayforcriticalquestionfour.pdf)
- **Products for Q3 and Q4:** Remediation and intervention strategies; "List of students with specific data"; "Student groupings by essential standard, learning target, or need"; "Monitoring tool" (progress after intervention); "Extension activities"; "Monitoring tool" after extension. — [PLC Products, Tasks, and Time Lines](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_HLPLCSI/plcproductstasksandtimelines.pdf)
- **Critical Issues item 13:** "We use the results of our common assessments to identify students who need additional time and support to master essential learning, and we work within the systems and processes of the school to ensure they receive that support." — [LBD4 Critical Issues](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/criticalissuesforteamconsideration.pdf)
- **Pyramid of interventions / MTSS:** Supplemental interventions in grade-level curriculum (Tier 2) and intensive interventions in foundational skills (Tier 3), "while maintaining their access to essential grade-level curriculum during core instruction." — [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf)

### Inferences
- An app response plan for each target should list: students (by need group), the specific misconception, the strategy, who leads it, when (classroom Tier 1 re-engagement vs. Tier 2 flexible time), reassessment item(s) and date, and the result after reassessment. The extension group needs an activity at a higher DOK.

- I found no single prescribed number of minutes or days per week for flexible time. Schools set this locally.

## 9. Norms, roles, agendas, minutes, and team products (incl. Critical Issues for Team Consideration)

### Takeaway
Every team writes norms and reviews them at each meeting, then evaluates them at least twice a year. Teams use roles (facilitator, recorder, timekeeper) and a three-part agenda (focus it / do it / review it). Teams keep written products: norms, SMART goals, essential standards, unwrapped targets, CFAs, rubrics, data analyses, intervention lists. The 18-item "Critical Issues for Team Consideration" self-rating is the LBD checklist of what a team must be able to say it has done.

### Cited Findings
- **Team norms definition:** "In PLCs, norms represent collective commitments developed by each team to guide members in working together." — [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf). On why: "Inattention to establishing specific team norms is one of the major reasons teams fail (Blanchard, 2007)." — [LBD4: Why Should We Create Norms?](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/whyshouldwecreatenorms.pdf)
- **Identifying Team Norms Protocol (from LBD 2016), 9 steps:**
  1. Explain the purpose. Areas to consider: "decision making, participation, time management, response to conflict, and confidentiality."
  2. Members individually list negative behaviors from past teams on sticky notes.
  3. Cluster them.
  4. Members list positive behaviors (different-colored notes).
  5. Cluster and link positive to negative behaviors.
  6. Discuss which to include.
  7. Use fist-to-five consensus.
  8. Agree how to handle norm violations ("care enough to confront").
  9. "Capture the final set of norms in writing… Start each meeting with a review of the norms, and plan to revisit them twice a year."
  — [Plan Book: Identifying Team Norms Protocol](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_CTPBPLC/identifyingteamnormsprotocol.pdf)
- **Sample norms:** "Be honest… Participate… Focus on the task… Treat one another as equals… Ensure equal airtime… Come to meetings ready to work and learn. Honor time limits and commitments." — [Taking Action: Sample Team Norms (adapted from Malone 2006)](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_TARTI2nd/sampleteamnorms.pdf)
- **Team Meeting Agenda Template:**
  - Roles: Facilitator, Recorder, Timekeeper, Other roles; plus a Team norms field.
  - **Focus it** (first 3–5 min): "Where are we in the PDSA cycle? What do we plan to accomplish today? What will we walk away having reached or created (for example, decisions, products, or a plan of action)? What process will we be using?"
  - **Do it** (most of the meeting): the facilitator guides, "the recorder takes notes on key decisions or products made," the timekeeper monitors time.
  - **Review it** (last 5 min): "Action steps and the responsible team member; Date of the next meeting; Agenda for the next meeting; Data or information to bring to the next meeting; Reflection on norms; Questions for an administrator."
  — [Plan Book: Team Meeting Agenda Template (adapted from Bailey & Jakicic 2019)](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_CTPBPLC/teammeetingagendatemplate.pdf)
- **Protocols definition:** "Agreed-on guidelines that promote efficient and effective conversations… a protocol typically defines roles and contains specific time frames for a series of prescribed steps." Consensus "is achieved when (1) all points of view have not only been heard but also solicited, and (2) the will of the group is evident even to those who most oppose it." — [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf)
- **Critical Issues for Team Consideration (LBD, ©2006–2024).** Teams rate each item 1–10 (Not True of Our Team / Addressing / True). The 18 items:
  1. norms and protocols
  2. SMART goals from analyzed data
  3. clarity on essential learning for the course and each unit
  4. alignment with state standards and high-stakes tests
  5. content to eliminate
  6. sequence and pacing guides
  7. prerequisite knowledge and skills identified
  8. instruments to assess prerequisites
  9. systems to help students acquire prerequisites
  10. frequent CFAs
  11. proficiency standard for each skill on CFAs
  12. using results to help each other build on strengths and address weaknesses
  13. using results to identify students for time and support
  14. agreed criteria for judging student work, practiced for consistency
  15. teaching students the criteria with examples
  16. common summative assessments
  17. proficiency standard for summative assessments
  18. "formally evaluate our adherence to team norms and the effectiveness of our team at least twice each year"
  — [LBD4: Critical Issues for Team Consideration](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/criticalissuesforteamconsideration.pdf)
- **PLC Products, Tasks, and Time Lines** (*How to Leverage PLCs for School Improvement*, 2015). Pairs each team task with a product:
  - Norms; Protocols (roles, agenda building, record keeping, consensus); SMART goals; SMART goal action plan; description of celebrations.
  - Q1: curriculum documents, essential standards list, aligned curriculum guides, pacing guide.
  - Q2: unwrapped standards with targets, assessment map, CFAs, proficiency rationale, rubric, student-friendly targets, anchor papers, item analysis.
  - Q3: remediation and intervention strategies, student lists, groupings, monitoring tool.
  - Q4: groupings, extension activities, student lists, monitoring tool.
  - Includes a "Completion Date" column.
  — [HLPLCSI: PLC Products, Tasks, and Time Lines](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_HLPLCSI/plcproductstasksandtimelines.pdf)
- **Team definition:** "A group of people working interdependently to achieve a common goal for which members are mutually accountable. Collaborative teams are the fundamental building blocks of PLCs." — [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf)

### Inferences
- The app's one-time "team setup" should capture: team name, members, norms (with the five LBD areas as prompts), roles (facilitator, recorder, timekeeper), and an optional Critical Issues self-rating. Each meeting record = focus, decisions/products, action items with owner, next agenda, data to bring, norm reflection.

- Some *Taking Action* tools ("Team Charter," "Team Action-Planning Template") were listed but not read.

## 10. Recommended sequence of a team learning cycle / unit cycle (steps in order)

### Takeaway
Several Solution Tree sources give the same order. Year-level setup: norms and SMART goal, then essential standards and pacing. Per unit (Plan-Do-Study-Act):
1. Identify the unit's essential standards.
2. Define the end-of-unit evidence and proficiency (with a unit SMART goal).
3. Unwrap into learning targets.
4. Choose which targets to assess with CFAs and design them.
5. Sequence instruction on a calendar with CFA and data-meeting dates.
6. Teach and give the CFAs.
7. Analyze by target, student and teacher.
8. Respond (intervene and extend).
9. Reassess.
10. Give the common end-of-unit assessment, check the goal, and reflect to refine the unit.

### Cited Findings
- **Glossary "team learning process" (LBD):** "clarifying essential student learnings… agreeing on common pacing of instruction; developing multiple common formative assessments aligned to each essential outcome; establishing specific, rigorous target scores or benchmarks…; analyzing common assessment results; and identifying and implementing improvement strategies. Teams address each step in the process by first building shared knowledge rather than pooling opinions." — [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf)
- **Continuous improvement cycle (AllThingsPLC FAQ):** "Gathering evidence of current levels of student learning; Developing strategies and ideas to build on strengths and address weaknesses in that learning; [implementing]; Analyzing the impact of the changes…; Applying the new knowledge in the next cycle." — [AllThingsPLC FAQ](https://allthingsplc.info/faq/); same list in the [LBD Glossary](https://allthingsplc.info/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Terms.pdf) ("continuous improvement process")
- **PDSA Cycle (Bailey & Jakicic 2019, in the Plan Book):** "guides the work of collaborative teams on a unit-by-unit basis."
  - **Year:** essential standards (Q1), examples of proficiency (Q2), pacing.
  - **Plan (each unit):** unwrap the unit's essential standards into targets (Q1); design summative and formative items and timing (Q2) "using SMART… goals for end-of-unit assessments"; sequence instruction (Q2.5).
  - **Do:** "implement… collect evidence of student learning at key times… through… common assessments."
  - **Study:** "identify general strengths and error patterns, specific student levels…, and effective practices… game plan to support students who do not achieve proficiency and… extending (Q3, Q4)… evaluate the effectiveness of the assessment items, pacing, and instructional strategies."
  - **Act:** "act on their game plan… gather additional evidence… to ensure that their support resulted in higher levels of student learning."
  — [Plan Book: The PDSA Cycle](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_CTPBPLC/thepdsacycle.pdf)
- **Five-Step Unit-Planning Template** (backward planning; Bailey & Jakicic 2019):
  - **Step 1** (Q1): "Identify the essential or power standards and the supporting standards for the unit."
  - **Step 2** (Q2): "Discuss evidence of the end in mind (end-of-unit measure)… What type of task…? With what level of proficiency…? And what type of problem or text (stimulus)…?" (Use released items and adjacent grade levels.)
  - **Step 3**: "Share the specific learning targets… Be sure to identify the big ideas…" Targets are split into know / do / academic vocabulary, each paired with assessment items.
  - **Step 4**: "Where in the unit does it make sense to see if our students are learning…? (Formative assessment)" Identify the targets to assess in common, focusing on the challenging ones, with brief aligned items and the expected proficiency.
  - **Step 5**: "Plan the sequence of instruction and the timing for common formative assessments." The table has columns Days, Lessons or Activities, Embedded Assessment Checkpoints.
  — [Plan Book: Five-Step Unit-Planning Template](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_CTPBPLC/five-stepunit-planningtemplate.pdf)
- **Taking Action Unit Assessment Plan (4 steps):**
  1. "Identify and deconstruct each essential standard to identify the learning targets for the unit."
  2. "Organize the learning targets into a sequenced learning progression…"
  3. "Collaboratively create or select common assessments to administer at the end of and throughout the unit."
  4. "Create a team calendar and agree on when to administer and respond to each common assessment." The calendar notes meeting days for analysis, response days, and the focus target for each meeting.
  — [Taking Action: Unit Assessment Plan](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_TARTI2nd/unitassessmentplan.pdf)
- **Before / During / After phases of common assessment:** Before = clarity on targets and a picture of proficiency (unwrapping). During = design aligned items and agree on proficiency before giving the assessment. After = analyze (proficient, exceeding, needing support), bring student work, plan interventions, and examine instructional and curricular effectiveness. — [Solution Tree Blog: Three Critical Phases](https://www.solutiontree.com/blog/?p=4874)
- **DuFour's 4-part collaborative work (2003):** "1) clarifying the essential knowledge and skills of a particular grade level, course, or unit of instruction; 2) developing common assessments of student learning; 3) analyzing results to identify areas of strength and weakness for both individual teachers and the team; and 4) establishing specific goals and action plans to improve student achievement." — [DuFour, JSD 2003](https://learningforward.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/dufour244.pdf)
- **Kanold "PLC Teaching-Assessing-Learning Cycle"** (*Common Core Mathematics in a PLC at Work*, Fig. 4.1). A 5-step math-team cycle: step 1 involves assessment instruments and feedback; step 4, "Students use the assessment instruments from step one for motivation, reflection, and action"; step 5, "Collaborative teams use ongoing assessment feedback to improve their instruction." — [search summary of AllThingsPLC upload](https://allthingsplc.secure.agroup.com/files/uploads/teaching-assessing-learning-cycle.pdf) (URL returned 404 when fetched; steps 2–3 not confirmed)
- **Pathways tools sequence (LBD4, from *Amplify Your Impact*):**
  - Q1: Prioritizing Standards, then Identifying Targets, then Determining Proficiency, then Planning Units, then Analyzing Strategies.
  - Q2: Creating CFAs, then Analyzing Student Work, then Analyzing Assessment Data.
  - Q3: Analyzing Strategies, then Reviewing Assessments, then Planning Classroom Interventions, then Utilizing a System of Supports.
  - Q4: Planning Enrichment Activities.
  — [Pathways Q1](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/pathwaystoolforcriticalquestionone.pdf), [Q2](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/pathwaysforcriticalquestiontwo.pdf), [Q3](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/pathwaysforcriticalquestionthree.pdf), [Q4](https://cloudfront-s3.solutiontree.com/pdfs/Reproducibles_LBD4/pathwayforcriticalquestionfour.pdf)
- **LBD chapter flow (from the LBD4 reproducibles index order):**
  1. Why PLC / cultural shifts
  2. Mission, vision, collective commitments, goals (foundation)
  3. Teams as the basic structure, collaboration and norms ("Critical Issues for Team Consideration")
  4. Coaching collaborative teams
  5. SMART goals / results orientation
  6. Guaranteed and viable curriculum (clarify what students must learn)
  7. Formative and common assessments (turning data into information)
  8. Systematic interventions and extensions
  9. Hiring and retaining staff
  10. Celebration, conflict, districtwide implementation
  — [LBD4 reproducibles index](https://www.solutiontree.com/free-resources/plcbooks/lbd4). The 4th edition (2024, 368 pp., adds Anthony Muhammad) also includes a closing chapter on a culture of caring. — [search summaries of the issuu look-inside and bookseller listings](https://issuu.com/mm905/docs/learning_by_doing_fourth_edition_look-inside)
- **Contrast with Data Teams (Leadership and Learning Center):** five steps — "collect and chart data, analyze strengths and obstacles, set goals, select instructional strategies, determine results indicators" — run inside a pre-assessment, then teach, then post-assessment cycle. The pre-assessment is explicit there. — search summaries ([CT SDE Data Teams desktop reference](https://test-cd.ct.gov/-/media/sde/srbi/dt_desktop_reference_guide.pdf); [Hilliard Data Team Flowchart](https://www.hilliardschools.org/pinwheel/assets/Data_Team_Flowchart.pdf)). PLC at Work instead puts standards, targets and CFA design first and the data meeting after the CFA. ### Inferences
- **Proposed "one complete plan" definition for the app**, synthesized from the sources above:
  - **Setup (once per year):** team, members, roles, norms; team SMART goal tied to school goal; essential standards chart with when-taught, prerequisites, proficiency example and extension.
  - **Unit:**
    1. Unit name and dates, with essential and supporting standards.
    2. End-of-unit common assessment and proficiency definition, plus a unit SMART goal (current reality, then target %).
    3. Unwrapped learning targets: know/do (or K/R/S/P), DOK, vocabulary, "I can" version, ordered simple to complex.
    4. Prerequisites, with an optional pre-check.
    5. CFA plan(s): targets assessed, item types and counts, proficiency cut per target, administration window, data-meeting date; ACID check.
    6. Calendar: lessons by day, CFA checkpoints, data meetings, response days.
    7. Scoring agreement and calibration.
    8. Data analysis record: by target, student and teacher; error groups; effective strategies.
    9. Response plan: intervention groups per target and need, plus an extension group; who, when (Tier 1 or Tier 2 time).
    10. Reassessment items and results.
    11. End-of-unit results against the SMART goal.
    12. Reflection: revise items, pacing and strategies; norms check.
  - A plan is "complete" when each of the four critical questions has a product attached: Q1 = standards plus targets; Q2 = CFA, proficiency criteria and data; Q3 = intervention plan plus reassessment; Q4 = extension plan.
