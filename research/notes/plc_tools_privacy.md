# PLC Software/Tools Landscape and Student-Data Privacy for PLC Meetings

Research method note: all findings come from web-search result content (snippets and search-tool summaries). WebFetch failed with DNS errors even for hosts on the stated allowlist (ascd.org, studentprivacy.ed.gov), so no page was read in full. Treat vendor claims as marketing. Research date: 2026-11-14.

## (A1) Which tools exist for PLCs, and what does each do?

### Takeaway
No single mainstream tool runs a full PLC cycle end to end. The market splits into (1) PD/content platforms (Solution Tree Global PD Teams), (2) assessment and data platforms with some PLC features (MasteryConnect, Illuminate/SchoolCity, Eduphoria Aware, Otus, Formative, Wayground, Progress Learning, ALL In Learning, Schoolytics), (3) generic collaboration shells with PLC templates (Microsoft Teams PLC team plus OneNote, Google Docs/Sheets, ClickUp, TpT binders), and (4) new AI-PLC tools (The Core Collaborative's AI-PLC Agent).

### Cited Findings
**PD/content platforms**
- Solution Tree Global PD Teams: a subscription PD platform for whole schools or districts built on Solution Tree author content (videos, books, study guides, reproducibles, custom playlists), with tools to assign content, track usage, and manage roles. Onboarding is included, and it supports Clever Instant Login. A single building license covers the whole faculty. Two subscription options exist, plus custom multi-year pricing, but no public price was found. Global PD subscribers were moved to Global PD Teams on July 1, 2022 — [Solution Tree GPDT best practices](https://www.solutiontree.com/globalpdteams/best-practices); [Annual subscription page](https://solutiontree.com/globalpdteams/annual-subscription); [Clever app gallery](https://www.clever.com/app-gallery/global-pd); marketing pages dated 2026 ([Feb 2026](https://mkt.solutiontree.com/l/77002/2026-02-02/crhbtq), [Apr 2026](https://mkt.solutiontree.com/l/77002/2026-04-23/crrtm8))
- A Solution Tree blog describes Global PD helping teams identify essential standards, then write and track CFAs by student and by learning target. This is a single testimonial-style article, undated in the results — [Solution Tree blog](https://www.solutiontree.com/blog/?p=2657)

**Assessment/data platforms**
- MasteryConnect (Instructure): standards-aligned assessment tracker. Users like the standards alignment, seeing which standards to reteach, sharing common assessments with teachers in the same subject, and the large ELA/math content bank. G2 rating is about 3.7 from 24 reviews. Another aggregator shows 3.6 from 9 reviews — [G2](https://g2.com/products/masteryconnect/reviews); [Common Sense Education reviews](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/masteryconnect/teacher-reviews/5033390); [SoftwareFinder](https://softwarefinder.com/lms/masteryconnect/reviews)
- Eduphoria (common in Texas): **Aware** handles test construction, item banks, administration, and analysis ("Quick Views", Single Test Analysis, constructed-response reports). Admin-built tests can be shared campus-wide. PLC collaboration sits in a separate product, **Strive**, which combines staff evaluation, goal setting, and PLC collaboration in one platform — [SoftwareReviews](https://www.softwarereviews.com/products/eduphoria); [CFBISD Aware resources](https://sites.google.com/a/cfbisd.edu/cfb-assessment-and-accountability/ctc-resou/local-assessments-aware); [Zoftwarehub](https://zoftwarehub.com/en-bh/products/eduphoria/overview)
- Illuminate Education (DnA, SchoolCity): positioned as "comprehensive assessment and MTSS collaboration and management tools". Gradebook data can be imported into SchoolCity, and DnA/SchoolCity assessments push scores to the Canvas gradebook via LTI. District guides list item, standard, and question-group reports plus misconception and error-pattern reports — [Illuminate partners/integrations](https://www.illuminateed.com/partners/partners-integrations/); [Illuminate](https://illuminateed.com/); [VESD district guide](https://sites.google.com/vesd.net/vesd-benchmarks/data-reports-anaylsis). One district page refers to "Renaissance (formally Illuminate)". This was not corroborated, so verify the ownership status.
- Otus: an assessment, data, and common-assessment platform with quote-based pricing and no public price. It has very few public reviews (SpotSaaS shows 1 rating). Otus announced a partnership with Discovery Education — [G2 pricing](https://www.g2.com/products/otus/pricing); [SpotSaaS](https://www.spotsaas.com/product/otus); [Otus common assessments guide](https://otus.com/guides/common-assessments/); [Otus/Discovery Education](https://otus.com/?p=75199)
- Formative: lesson delivery, assessment, and data analysis in one place, marketed with PLC collaboration and "common assessments best practices" help docs — [Formative PLC article](https://www.formative.com/read/professional-learning-communities); [Formative help](https://help.formative.com/en/articles/11584065-common-assessments-best-practices)
- Wayground (formerly Quizizz) markets a common-assessment product and publishes PLC content — [Wayground common assessment](https://wayground.com/home/common-assessment); [Wayground DuFour pitfalls blog](https://wayground.com/blog/3-plc-pitfalls-according-to-dufour)
- ALL In Learning: captures scores through a teacher app and says data reaches PLCs in near real time, so meetings go to analysis rather than collection — [ALL In Learning](https://www.allinlearning.com/?p=1128)
- Progress Learning: aligns CFAs to state standards and pitches less time spent "opening systems and filtering spreadsheets". It offers a free PLC data-analysis template — [Progress Learning routines](https://progresslearning.com/news-blog/establishing-data-analysis-routines/); [free template](https://progresslearning.com/news-blog/analyzing-data-plc-free-template/)
- CommonLit: standards-tagged questions let PLCs see which students struggle on which standards — [CommonLit blog](https://commonlit.org/blog/how-to-leverage-your-professional-learning-community/)
- Derivita (math) documents "PLC features" in its support site — [Derivita support](https://support.derivita.com/how-to-use-plc-features?hsLang=en)
- Schoolytics: a Google for Education partner that layers analytics on Google Classroom. It combines SIS, LMS, and Classroom assessment data in one dashboard and is built on Google Cloud/BigQuery. A free teacher tier allows up to 10 Classrooms (per a July 2023 document, so possibly outdated). Hashtag "assignment tags" in Classroom descriptions roll up performance by tag. It publishes a PLC meeting template. A 2020 figure says it served 250+ schools (outdated) — [Schoolytics PLC template](https://info.schoolytics.com/plc-meeting-template); [Google Cloud case study](https://cloud.google.com/customers/schoolytics); [Alice Keeler](https://alicekeeler.com/?p=32019); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/274892/schoolytics/reviews/); [BusinessWire 2020](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20201013005176/en/Schoolytics-Empowers-Educators-to-Make-Data-Driven-Decisions-and-Increase-Student-Success)

**Generic collaboration shells and templates**
- Microsoft Teams has a "Professional Learning Community (PLC)" team type. Creating one auto-generates a PLC OneNote notebook "with templates based on the DuFour model". Unlike Staff notebooks, a PLC notebook has **no private sections and no leader-only section: every member has full access to every section**. A Power BI tab is suggested for visualizing student data — [Microsoft Support: Create a PLC team](https://support.microsoft.com/en-US/education/create-a-plc-team-in-microsoft-teams); [Microsoft Learn: PLC notebooks](https://docs.microsoft.com/en-gb/learn/modules/collaborate-colleagues-live-teams-meetings-onenote/collaborate-peers-plc-notebooks); [Microsoft Learn: learner concerns](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/modules/collaborate-colleagues-staff-plc-teams-microsoft-teams/modify-plc-team-learner-concerns)
- TpT "PLC Data & Planning Bundle": a team binder (agendas, notes, SMART goal tracking, data discussion templates, action steps) plus a Google Sheets data tracker with pre-built formulas and printable PDFs, listed at $9.00 (down from $11.98) — [TpT listing](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/Product/PLC-Data-Planning-Bundle-15957913)
- Free templates: the AllThingsPLC meeting agenda PDF, ClickUp's PLC meeting notes template, Apple's PLC agenda template, and many district Google Sites with Docs/Sheets templates. One team has built a custom web form that writes PLC minutes to Google Sheets via Apps Script — [AllThingsPLC agenda](https://allthingsplc.secure.agroup.com/files/uploads/meeting_plc-agenda.pdf); [ClickUp](https://clickup.com/templates/meeting-notes/professional-learning-committee); [Apple](https://atlc-prod-rno.apple.com/resource/250012932); [Rawafid PLC templates](https://sites.google.com/rawafidschool.com/plc-at-rawafid/templates); [plcminutes example](https://plcminutes.pages.dev/)

**AI-PLC tools (newest)**
- The Core Collaborative's AI-PLC Agent (Paul Bloomberg and Isaac Wells) is billed as "the first AI tool in education purpose-built for PLCs". It automates an "Analysis of Evidence" process by organizing student work and self-assessment data against success criteria. A public app launch was announced for January 2026 (not confirmed as having happened). Orting SD (WA) runs an "AI-PLC Lab", with pilots in HI, WA, and NY. For teams without app approval, the vendor offers a custom Gemini Gem — [Core Collaborative](https://thecorecollaborative.com/?p=7156); [AI-PLC agent info](https://go.thecorecollaborative.com/ai-plc-agent-info)
- WestEd offers yearlong GenAI coaching for math PLCs. A New Teacher Center RCT of AI-feedback PLCs is registered to start September 2026 (no results yet) — [WestEd](https://www.wested.org/support/genai-coaching-for-math-plcs/); [AEA Social Science Registry](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/18950)

### Inferences
- In the data platforms, PLC features are mostly "share an assessment and view reports". The planning, meeting log, SMART goal, and intervention-tracking parts of the cycle live elsewhere, usually in Docs/OneNote/binders.
- Eduphoria bundling PLC collaboration with staff *evaluation* (Strive) is a design choice a privacy- and trust-focused app should deliberately avoid.
- Google-native options are thin. Schoolytics (Classroom analytics) and templates exist, but nothing found is a guided Google-based PLC cycle app. This looks like an open niche.

### Gaps
- Could not verify Branching Minds, Panorama, Kami, Edulastic, "PLC Associates", or "TeamUp" PLC features. Searches returned nothing specific, and these appear to be MTSS/annotation/assessment tools rather than PLC-cycle tools.
- No public pricing found for Global PD Teams, Otus, MasteryConnect, Illuminate, or Eduphoria. All appear to be quote-based district sales.
- No Notion PLC template evidence found.

## (A2) What do tools do well and poorly? Is there a step-by-step cycle guide, and is anonymous strategy sharing supported?

### Takeaway
Praise centers on automatic standards-aligned data and reteach lists. Complaints center on confusing usability, hard filtering, low-quality shared items, multiple-choice bias, and dependence on implementation. No tool found walks a team through every step of a PLC cycle. No tool found offers invitation-based or anonymous sharing of instructional strategies tied to results. The AI-PLC Agent comes closest on structured evidence analysis.

### Cited Findings
- MasteryConnect complaints: "ease of use can be quite confusing at times"; data filtering and navigating detailed results is difficult; a red/yellow/green mastery status reflects only the *last* assessment on a standard, not a combined score; there are "initial usability challenges" and limited effectiveness without integrations — [G2](https://g2.com/products/masteryconnect/reviews); [SpotSaaS](https://www.spotsaas.com/product/masteryconnect); [SelectHub](https://www.selecthub.com/p/assessment-software/masteryconnect/)
- One teacher says MasteryConnect "is not a site that will become part of my PLC" because shared assessments "are not of high quality" and lean toward "scan-tron, fill-in-the-bubble, regurgitation-type tests" — [Common Sense Education](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/masteryconnect/teacher-reviews/5033390)
- Implementation matters: a reviewer has seen MasteryConnect "implemented beautifully and horribly, to no fault of Mastery Connect" — [G2](https://g2.com/products/masteryconnect/reviews)
- Vendors' own pitches name the pain point: time lost collecting data, opening systems, and filtering spreadsheets before PLC meetings — [ALL In Learning](https://www.allinlearning.com/?p=1128); [Progress Learning](https://progresslearning.com/news-blog/establishing-data-analysis-routines/)
- A Kappan article argues PLCs see plenty of student-outcome data but little data on adult practice: "an abundance of information about effects and few clues about causes." This supports linking strategies to results — [Kappan](https://kappanonline.org/?p=123502)
- DuFour training material warns that "PLC" is often misapplied to "any weekly meeting" where data is discussed. Wayground summarizes "3 PLC pitfalls according to DuFour" — [SlideShare (DuFour-based)](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/principal-plc-presentation-68-k8-version/18088474); [Wayground](https://wayground.com/blog/3-plc-pitfalls-according-to-dufour)
- Microsoft's PLC notebook is DuFour-template-based, but it is a static notebook with no workflow enforcement — [Microsoft Learn](https://docs.microsoft.com/en-gb/learn/modules/collaborate-colleagues-live-teams-meetings-onenote/collaborate-peers-plc-notebooks)
- Integrations seen: Clever SSO (Global PD), Canvas LTI gradebook passback and gradebook import (Illuminate/SchoolCity), Google Classroom API (Schoolytics), and Power BI tabs (Teams) — sources above.

### Inferences
- Opportunities for PLC Launchpad: (1) a guided cycle (essential standard → CFA → data → strategy → intervention → reassess) rather than a blank notebook; (2) import from Google Forms quiz results, Sheets, or CSV so no data platform is needed; (3) capture *strategies* (adult practice) alongside results; (4) keep it simple, since usability is the top complaint; (5) keep PLC data strictly separate from evaluation.
- Mastery displays should not rely only on the last score, given the MasteryConnect complaint.

### Gaps
- No product found that guides a team through the whole cycle step by step. Absence in search is not proof none exists.
- No evidence found of anonymous or invitation-based strategy sharing in any PLC tool.
- Review samples are small (under 30 reviews per tool) and are mostly older than 2024.

## (B1) FERPA: can teammates see each other's student-level CFA results?

### Takeaway
Generally yes, within limits. FERPA's school-official exception lets teachers access education records without consent when they have a "legitimate educational interest" as defined in the district's annual FERPA notice. Schools must use "reasonable methods" to limit access to records each official needs. No federal guidance addresses PLCs specifically. Teaching or intervening with the same students, or co-owning a common course and assessment, is the usual justification, but this depends on local policy.

### Cited Findings
- Teachers are listed among "school officials" — [studentprivacy.ed.gov FAQ: Who is a school official](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/faq/who-school-official-under-ferpa)
- The standard test: an official has a legitimate educational interest "if the official needs to review an education record in order to fulfill his or her professional responsibility". Schools must state their criteria for "school official" and "legitimate educational interest" in the annual FERPA notification — [studentprivacy.ed.gov FAQ](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/faq/who-school-official-under-ferpa); [Student Privacy Compass](https://studentprivacycompass.org/leas); [CRS IF13155, Jan 2026](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF13155.html)
- Schools "must use reasonable methods to ensure that school officials obtain access to only those education records in which they have legitimate educational interests" (physical, technological, or administrative controls) — [studentprivacy.ed.gov FAQ](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/faq/what-must-educational-agencies-or-institutions-do-ensure-only-school-officials-legitimate) (seen via search only; the page fetch failed)
- FERPA "does not say who those persons are, nor ... how to determine the limits of a legitimate educational interest". It may need case-by-case determination using written criteria. This is older NCES guidance from 2004 — [NCES Forum Guide](https://nces.ed.gov/pubs2004/privacy/section_4b.asp)
- Educators may discuss student performance with colleagues who have a legitimate educational interest, but a "measured approach" is recommended — [Student Privacy Compass scenarios](https://studentprivacycompass.org/scenarios-communication)
- A 2026 CRS explainer summarizes FERPA and its exceptions (current) — [CRS IF13155](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-01-15_IF13155_3cc5e2b15cb28b759b6c19c0740e794eeb7161a7.html)

### Inferences
- An app should let each school record its own FERPA basis, restrict access to the team's members using school Google accounts, and default to showing a teacher identified student rows only for their own students. Peers should see aggregated or ID-only data unless the team co-serves those students, for example in shared intervention groups.
- Contrast with the Microsoft Teams PLC notebook, where every member sees every section. That is fine for planning, but storing named student data there exposes it to the whole team.
- Teacher-level data such as "Teacher A's class average" is also sensitive personnel information under state law and local contracts, separate from FERPA.

### Gaps
- No district PLC handbook was found that explicitly addresses FERPA for CFA data sharing. The Broward and Arkansas DESE sources were not reachable.
- State student-privacy laws such as SOPIPA-style vendor laws, plus app-vendor DPAs, were not researched in detail.

## (B2) Norms for teacher-by-teacher data without it becoming evaluative

### Takeaway
DuFour-school PLC guidance says leaders should commit that common assessment data will not be used punitively to evaluate teachers, formally or informally. Comparison exists to help each teacher learn from colleagues. Many teachers are still reluctant and prefer aggregate views. Critics say comparing teachers can turn PLCs into competition.

### Cited Findings
- DuFour, as quoted in a "PLCs and Data" document: leaders should "make commitments to the staff that common assessment data will not be used in a punitive way to formally or informally evaluate teacher performance" — [Scribd: PLCs and Data](https://www.scribd.com/document/441100774/PLCs-and-Data-pdf) (secondary source; verify in *Learning by Doing*)
- DuFour-based training: common assessments "inform individual teacher practice – provides teachers with a basis of comparison regarding the achievement of their students" — [SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/principal-plc-presentation-68-k8-version/18088474)
- Central Rivers AEA's PLC implementation map notes many teachers are "reluctant to share individual teacher results" and prefer to analyze aggregate group performance. The map is from 2017, so older — [Central Rivers AEA IC Map](https://prevmain.centralriversaea.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/M11_PLC-Focusing-on-Results-Part-II-IC-Map-Revised-6.2.17.pdf)
- Critique: a 2008 teacher account on Alfie Kohn's site describes a DuFour-style process that identified who taught a concept "best". The school "decided to reject the DuFour model". This is a single anecdote — [Alfie Kohn](https://www.alfiekohn.org/?p=1809)
- Otus notes that classroom differences make it "incredibly difficult to get an accurate point of comparison to measure ... teacher effectiveness" from common assessments — [Otus guide](https://otus.com/guides/common-assessments/)
- ASCD's *Educational Leadership* has an article "How PLCs Do Data Right". It was found in search but could not be fetched, so the content is unverified — [ASCD](https://ascd.org/el/articles/how-plcs-do-data-right)

### Inferences
- Design features that support non-evaluative use: (a) teacher-level breakdowns visible only to the team, not to admins by default, or optionally anonymized ("Class A/B/C") with self-reveal; (b) framing as "which strategy worked" rather than "which teacher"; (c) a team norms step at cycle start that includes a data-use agreement; (d) no export of teacher rankings.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve AllThingsPLC or Solution Tree articles directly on data and trust (fetch blocked). Primary DuFour/Eaker/Many text was not verified.
- No research found on anonymized teacher-level displays in PLCs specifically.

## (B3) Best practices: IDs vs names, aggregation, minimum-n, retention, account restrictions

### Takeaway
Federal and state disclosure-avoidance practice suppresses small cells, with thresholds of at least 3 (absolute minimum), commonly 5 or 10. It also uses complementary suppression and guards against extreme percentages. These rules are written for public reporting, not internal teams, but they offer defensible defaults for any view shared beyond the students' own teachers.

### Cited Findings
- PTAC/disclosure guidance: small unrounded counts are not automatically a disclosure, "but the smaller the cell, the greater the disclosure risk". Many statisticians treat 3 as the absolute minimum, with 5 or 10 for more protection — [CT disclosure guidance](https://www.ct.edu/dataprivacy/disclosure); [Georgia DOE / US ED Disclosure Avoidance Basics (FY2020)](https://georgiainsights.gadoe.org/Data-Collections/Documents/DC_Conf_US%20ED/FY2020/Disclosure%20Avoidance%20Basics.pdf)
- Thresholds in practice: Maryland MHEC suppresses groups of 0–9 (n≥10); Connecticut EdSight suppresses cells ≤5; DC OSSE suppresses counts 0–9 and extreme percentages for larger denominators (2022 policy) — [MHEC](https://mhec.maryland.gov/publications/Documents/Research/MHECSuppressionPolicy_Old.pdf); [CT EdSight](https://edsight.ct.gov/relatedreports/BDCRE%20Data%20Suppression%20Rules.pdf); [DC OSSE 2022](https://osse.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/osse/publication/attachments/OSSE%20Student%20Privacy%20and%20Data%20Suppression%20Policy_FINAL_1.27.22.pdf)
- Complementary suppression is needed so hidden values can't be derived from totals. Near-0% or near-100% cells can disclose even when larger — [CT disclosure avoidance](https://portal.ct.gov/-/media/sde/performance/data-requests/ctdisclosureavoidance082014.pdf)
- DC OSSE also has a separate *educator workforce* data suppression policy, a precedent for protecting teacher-level data — [OSSE workforce policy 2022](https://osse.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/osse/publication/attachments/OSSE%20Educational%20Workforce%20Privacy%20and%20Data%20Suppression%20Policy%20FINAL%201.27.22.pdf)
- Data Quality Campaign: n-size choices trade off privacy and visibility of small subgroups (2017, older) — [DQC N-size paper](https://dataqualitycampaign.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/DQC-N-size-paper-FINAL.pdf)
- ED's PTAC publishes LEA transparency best practices for telling families about data use — [LEA Transparency Best Practices](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/sites/default/files/resource_document/file/LEA%20Transparency%20Best%20Practices%20final.pdf)

### Inferences
- Suggested app defaults: use student IDs or initials in team-wide views and full names only to the student's own teacher. Suppress or merge subgroup cells under n=5 (or 10) in any cross-class or admin view, and never show a 100%/0% subgroup with small n. Restrict sign-in to the school's Google Workspace domain. Store data in school-owned Drive/Sheets, not personal accounts. Set a retention default, such as archiving or deleting student-level rows at year end and keeping only aggregates and strategies. Log who viewed what.
- Minimum-n thresholds come from public-reporting contexts. Applying them inside a team is a prudent practice, not a FERPA mandate.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source found on recommended *retention periods* for PLC/CFA working data. These are typically set by state records-retention schedules (not researched).
- No district guidance found specifically on IDs vs names in PLC meetings.
- Google Workspace for Education data-protection terms were not researched.
